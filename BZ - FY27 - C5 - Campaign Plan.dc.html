Why this sector

Why WA local government

The pressure

Under section 5.38 of the Local Government Act 1995, a council that employs a CEO for more than a year must formally review their performance at least once every year. Governance and corporate services teams running that process are usually small, and doing it in a way that meets the state's minimum standards — fair, evidence-based, properly recorded — on top of budget season, an election cycle and normal Council business is hard to do well without support.

The opportunity

An independent review that is procedurally sound, defensible and takes the load off internal staff — KRA design, survey administration, evidence gathering, remuneration benchmarking and the final report — while Council keeps ownership of the KRAs and the endorsement.

Market shape

Fragmented and geographically dispersed. WA has 139 local governments, from CBD councils to remote shires, most without in-house capacity to run an independent, arm's-length review. Target councils where the CEO's review is due, overdue, or contested, and where the outcome feeds into a remuneration decision.

Review-ready focus areas to lead with
KRA design & sign-off Elected member survey Staff survey (optional) SAT remuneration benchmarking Confidential final report to Council

Instant expert

Talk like you understand their world

A five-minute brief so you can talk to a Mayor, President or CEO like someone who understands local government — not a caller reading a script.

The governance environment they operate in

Council — the elected body — sets strategic direction and manages the CEO's performance; the Mayor or President chairs meetings but has no unilateral authority; the CEO runs the administration. WALGA is the peak body for all 139 WA councils; the Department of Local Government, Sport and Cultural Industries (DLGSC) sets the minimum standards the review must meet.

Why the review carries weight

It is a statutory obligation (ss 5.38–5.39), it sets remuneration within the Salaries & Allowances Tribunal band, and a flawed process can be challenged. Councils want a fair, transparent, minute-worthy record — not just a conversation.

The pressures shaping their year

Ordinary elections are held every two years, on the third Saturday in October, and reshape half of Council — often delaying or resetting a review. The financial year runs July–June, driving budget season around the same time as many CEO anniversaries, and every material decision, including endorsing the assessment, needs an absolute majority resolution at a scheduled meeting.

Where independent review fits

Council keeps the KRAs and the final endorsement. Blue Zoo runs the process — survey design, evidence gathering, benchmarking and drafting — impartially and on the record, so the outcome is defensible whichever way it lands.

Talk like an insider

Know the language: KRAs, absolute majority, the Salaries & Allowances Tribunal (SAT), the Act, minimum standards S2.1–S2.6, Elected Members. Know the calendar: financial year end, the October election cycle, and the standing committee dates that decisions have to land on.

Objection handling

Acknowledge, reframe, ask

Objection Response angle
"We run this ourselves."Council keeps the KRAs and the endorsement — we run the impartial part: evidence gathering and the survey. That's the part hardest to keep independent in-house.
"We're a small or rural council."The minimum standards apply regardless of size — a proportionate, light process still meets s5.38 in full.
"It's not due yet."Worth locking the date now so KRAs are agreed in writing before the review window opens — when is it next due?
"Council will handle it at the next meeting."Happy to work to your meeting calendar — what would need to be on the agenda paper to get this moving?
"We tried an external reviewer before."What didn't work? Our process is built directly to the Department's Standards & Guidelines, with the evidence trail to match.
"Budget's already set."Is there provision for a governance or professional-services line, or could this sit within an existing budget?
"Send me some information."Happy to — is the priority the KRA process, evidence gathering, or the remuneration review? I'll send the matching brief.
Know when to stop

A clear "no" frees time for a better-fit council. Close the loop courteously, set a nurture date if there's a future trigger, and respect every opt-out immediately.

Appendix A · reference framework

CEO performance review framework

The review Blue Zoo delivers is built around the WA model standards for local government CEO performance review — grounded in the Local Government Act 1995 (ss 5.38–5.39) and the Department's Standards & Guidelines.

Principles

The performance review rests on three principles: fairness, integrity and impartiality.

Annual requirement

For a CEO employed more than one year, performance must be reviewed formally at least once every year (s 5.38).

The minimum standard · met if
S2.1 Key result areas are specific, relevant, measurable, achievable and time-based.
S2.2 KRAs and the process are recorded in writing, negotiated with and agreed by the CEO and Council.
S2.3 The CEO is told how performance will be managed and the results of the assessment.
S2.4 Evidence gathered against the KRAs is thorough and comprehensive.
S2.5 Assessment is free from bias, based on achievement against KRAs; decisions are impartial, transparent and reviewable.
S2.6 Council has endorsed the assessment by absolute majority.
Key result areas
Service delivery vs Strategic Community Plan · Budget compliance · Organisational capability · Operational & project management · Financial performance & asset management · Timely, accurate advice to councillors · Implementation of council resolutions · Management of organisational risk · Leadership, conduct & HR management · Stakeholder management & satisfaction
Sources of evidence
Key business outcomes achieved · Progress on the council's strategic vision · Audit & risk committee reports · Workforce metrics & incident reports · Organisational survey results · Stakeholder & union relationships

Source: Department of Local Government, Sport & Cultural Industries — Standards & Guidelines for Local Government CEO Recruitment and Selection, Performance Review and Termination (Part 2 — Performance Review). Reproduced for internal reference.

Appendix B · engagement process map

How a review is delivered

Once a council engages Blue Zoo, the work follows a proven appraisal methodology — 17 steps grouped into four phases, converging on a single confidential report to Council.

Phase 1 · Set up & align
1 Brief the Mayor, Deputy Mayor & CEO — agree method, roles & timeline
2 Issue current KPIs to the CEO with an invitation to prepare a summary report
3 Request CEO remuneration data from Finance
Phase 2 · Build & release surveys
4 Draft the staff survey, behavioural focus — optional
5 Draft the Elected Member survey — KPI results, CEO comments & behavioural questions
6 Staff survey approved by Mayor & Deputy Mayor (if run)
7 Release the Elected Member survey
8 Release the staff survey to staff (if run)
Phase 3 · Survey & remuneration window
9 Final date for remuneration data from Finance
10 Survey period runs (staff & Elected Members)
11 Remuneration review — SAT band, package breakdown & market benchmarking
12 Survey period closes
Phase 4 · Analyse, report & endorse
13 Commence data analysis & report drafting
14 Draft report to Mayor, Deputy Mayor & CEO to comment
15 Integrate comments into the final report draft
16 Present the final report draft
17 Refer to Governance for the next Council agenda (closed)

Simplified from Blue Zoo's 17-step Performance Appraisal Methodology. Every stage is dependent on the availability of councillors and third parties and the schedule of Council meetings; all activity is conducted on a confidential basis.
