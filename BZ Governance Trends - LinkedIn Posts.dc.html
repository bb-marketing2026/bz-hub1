Post 1 · Cyber resilience

Attacks on Australian critical infrastructure rose 111% last year. Proactive threat warnings to entities were up 83%. The defensive effort is real — but the threat is scaling faster. For boards, cyber is no longer an ICT line item; it is a standing accountability. The right question at the table isn't "are we compliant?" — it's "if this happens to us, how would we know, and how fast could we respond?" How ready is your agency?

Post 2 · Performance audit

Two-thirds of this year's WA information-systems audit findings were repeats of issues raised in prior years — and more than half of those are rated significant. A finding that recurs is no longer a technical gap; it is a governance signal. It tells the Auditor General that oversight isn't translating into action. Agencies that prepare for performance audits the way they prepare for financial ones close findings before they become a pattern. Would last year's findings still be open in yours?

Post 3 · AI governance

70% of public servants now use AI in their daily work — up from 58% a year ago. Confidence is climbing too. Adoption is not the problem. Oversight is. Most agencies can't yet name where AI is used, who is accountable for it, or how an AI-assisted decision would be explained. The APS AI Plan now expects a Chief AI Officer and clear accountability in every agency. The board question is simple: can we explain, defend and account for how we use AI?

Post 4 · Integrity

Since 2023 the NACC has received more than 6,000 referrals, assessed 85% of them, and secured 11 convictions. State commissions are just as active. The tolerance for opaque decision-making has fallen sharply. What protects an executive today is not the absence of scrutiny — it's a documented, defensible trail showing how a decision was reached and how conflicts were managed. If your decisions were examined tomorrow, would the record speak for you?

Post 5 · Culture

The good news from the 2025 APS Census: 81% of staff say their agency supports acting with integrity — up five points — and 91% are willing to go the extra mile. Culture is the quiet foundation of good governance. Controls set the floor; culture determines what happens when no one is watching. The gap to watch is innovation: only half feel safe to treat failure as part of learning. Governance that only punishes failure gets compliance. Governance that enables good judgement gets performance.

Post 6 · The cost of getting it wrong

The average cost of a cyber incident to an Australian business rose 50% in a year to $80,850 — and to $202,700 for large organisations. For a public agency the true cost is rarely just financial. It's the disruption to services, the erosion of community trust, and the adverse finding that follows. Prevention is almost always cheaper than recovery — and far cheaper than the reputational bill. Good governance is the most cost-effective control you have.
