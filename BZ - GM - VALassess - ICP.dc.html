The campaign

Could you defend your valuation methodology to a buyer's due diligence?

Finance leaders own the numbers that determine what the organisation is worth — in a transaction, a fundraising round, an impairment test or a set of statutory accounts. What is rarely governed is the process that produces those numbers: which methodology, applied consistently by whom, on what assumptions, reviewed by which independent party.

This campaign offers finance leadership an objective read on valuation governance using the VALassess Framework in Governance Manager. It is deliberately not a valuation service pitch — we are not offering to value anything. We are offering to measure one domain of the valuation process — whichever they are least comfortable about — which keeps the commitment to an hour and the conversation to a single, contained question.

Value proposition

“Understand and govern the key value drivers — so the valuation withstands scrutiny and reflects what the business is genuinely worth.”

The offer

A free assessment against one domain of the VALassess Framework — their choice of the four.

The call to action

Assess one domain in under an hour, then a 30‑minute debrief with a Blue Zoo adviser.

Why this lands with finance leaders

It speaks their language. Finance leaders are comfortable with methodology, assumptions, consistency and independent review. Governance framed that way reads as rigour, not overhead.

The stakes are unusually concrete. A weak valuation process shows up as a discount at transaction, an audit qualification, a restated impairment, or a fundraising round priced against you.

It anticipates due diligence. Buyers and investors interrogate valuation process before they accept a number. Seeing what they will see, early, is straightforwardly useful.

One domain is an easy yes. A whole-framework assessment reads as a project. One domain, chosen by them, reads as an hour — and it is the single biggest driver of conversion in this campaign.

Positioning discipline: never question their numbers. A CFO will disengage instantly from anything implying the valuation is wrong. The premise is that the number is sound and the process around it is unmeasured — which is a governance gap, not a competence gap.

Section one

Ideal customer profile

The best fit is an organisation where valuation genuinely matters — a transaction in prospect, material assets to appraise, or external parties who price the business — and where a finance leader is personally accountable for the methodology.

Attribute
Ideal profile
Sector
Private and family companies approaching sale or succession, ASX small and mid‑cap, private equity portfolio companies, mutuals and co‑operatives, member funds, statutory authorities with material asset bases, utilities, infrastructure
Size
Revenue $20m–$1bn, or an asset base above $50m. Large enough for valuation to be consequential, small enough that a Big Four valuation practice is not already embedded
Structure
A CFO or Finance Director with a finance team; an audit committee or board finance committee; external audit in place
Valuation context
Recurring valuation work — impairment testing, asset revaluation, share valuations for ESOP or buy‑sell, transaction or fundraising activity, or fair‑value reporting under AASB 13
Trigger events
Sale, merger or capital raise in prospect; succession planning; a new CFO appointed; an audit finding on valuation or impairment; an ESOP or buy‑sell agreement being written; ESG reporting obligations arriving
Disqualifiers
No material valuation activity; valuation wholly outsourced with no internal methodology role; a transaction already in exclusivity (too late to change process)

Buying roles

Primary — the champion

CFO, Finance Director, Director Finance & Corporate Services. Owns the methodology, carries the exposure, and can authorise a free baseline without procurement.

Secondary champion

Finance Manager or Financial Controller. Often does the valuation work itself and feels the inconsistency most acutely — an excellent route in when the CFO is unreachable.

Influencer — assurance

Audit committee chair, Chief Audit Executive, external auditor. Consistently supportive, because a governed valuation process reduces audit effort and qualification risk.

Blocker to expect

A CFO who hears “valuation” and assumes we are pitching a valuation service or questioning their numbers. Correct this in the first two sentences, every time.

The finance leader, in their own terms

CFOs are analytical, sceptical and heavily prospected. They respond to precision and to consequences they can quantify — and they disengage immediately from anything that sounds like a generic governance pitch.

What keeps them awake
A buyer's adviser dismantling the methodology in diligence
Two business units valuing similar assets differently
Key assumptions held in one analyst's spreadsheet
An impairment call that has to be restated
An audit query on fair value they can't close quickly
ESG factors moving value with no framework to price them
What they want instead
One documented methodology applied consistently
Assumptions recorded, owned and independently reviewed
A valuation file that survives diligence without rework
Faster, cleaner audit conversations on fair value
Value drivers understood well enough to manage, not just report
A defensible position when the number is challenged

Objections and honest answers

Objection
Response
“We use external valuers.”
Good — and their work still relies on your data, your assumptions and your instructions. VALassess measures the governance around that, which remains yours regardless of who runs the model.
“Our auditors sign it off.”
An audit tests whether the outcome is materially correct this year. Maturity assessment tests whether the process would produce a defensible answer consistently — a different and forward‑looking question.
“Are you questioning our numbers?”
No. We assess the process, not the valuation. We never produce a competing number and never comment on your conclusion.
“I don't have time.”
Under an hour, no model access and no data extracts. It typically saves more than that in the next audit or diligence cycle.
“What does it cost afterwards?”
VALassess starts from $2,016 for assessment packs, scaling by number of assessments and access period. Published, transparent pricing.

Section three

What VALassess actually measures

Four governance capabilities, each scored for maturity and for the confidence with which it could be evidenced — so a finance leader can see both how strong a practice is and how well it would stand up to scrutiny.

Standardised practice
Valuation methodologies are standardised across the parts of the organisation that use them, producing consistent and reliable outcomes rather than local variation.
Regulatory compliance
Valuation practice is monitored against current financial regulation and reporting standards, mitigating the risk of non‑compliance and misreporting.
Factor integration
A broad spectrum of factors — including ESG considerations — is integrated into the valuation process, giving a more complete and accurate picture of company value.
Capability & training
The people performing valuations have the expertise to handle complex scenarios and changes in valuation standards — capability, not just process.

The outcomes we can credibly promise

Confidence among stakeholders

Robust, accurate and transparent valuation process underpins investor and stakeholder confidence, and protects credibility in the market.

Better strategic decisions

Internal decisions on mergers, acquisitions and fundraising rest on a valuation process the board can rely on.

Reduced misreporting risk

Governed methodology and monitored compliance materially reduce the risk associated with financial misreporting.

Greater agility

A standardised process adapts faster to market change and to shifting valuation standards than one rebuilt each cycle.

Campaign mechanics

A four‑week sequence per cohort, run by the BDM with Pat Scally on the debriefs. Everything is tracked in HubSpot against the standard deal stages.

Week 1
Reach

Intro email to the cohort; LinkedIn posts 1–3; connection requests to CFOs and Finance Directors.

Week 2
Engage

Follow‑up email; posts 4–6; connected calls to book domain assessments. Minimum 20 connected calls.

Week 3
Assess

Domain assessments run; posts 7–9; profiles produced and debriefs scheduled.

Week 4
Convert

30‑minute debriefs; posts 10–12; proposals for ongoing assessment packs.

One cohort per month. Time cohorts to the reporting calendar — approach three to four months before year‑end, while methodology can still be changed for the current cycle. CPA and CA ANZ networks are the fastest routes to a warm list.
