Two things at the outset, because both assumptions are reasonable: this isn't salary benchmarking, and I'm not suggesting anything is wrong with how [Organisation] pays people.

What I'd like to raise is the governance around remuneration. When a pay decision is questioned — by an employee, a tribunal, an auditor, or a remuneration committee — the question is almost never “is this number right?” It's “on what documented basis was it set?” Which framework, applied by whom, against which criteria, reviewed against what.

Most HR and People leaders are confident their pay decisions are made fairly and in good faith. Far fewer can produce an evidenced account of the framework behind them, because remuneration governance is rarely measured as a discipline in its own right.

The REMassess Framework in Governance Manager measures exactly that, across five domains: structured remuneration frameworks, compliance management, performance‑linked compensation, equity and benchmarking, and analytics and reporting. Each is scored for maturity and for how readily it could be evidenced.

I'd like to offer you a free assessment against one domain of the framework — whichever you choose. Practically:

  • Under an hour of your time, on the single domain you're least comfortable about
  • No pay data required — we assess the governance, not individual salaries
  • A maturity profile on that domain and a prioritised gap list, in writing
  • Confidential to you — nothing goes to your executive or board unless you send it

Two places it tends to earn its hour quickly: the next remuneration committee paper where you're asked to justify the framework, and the next reporting cycle where a pay figure becomes public and someone asks how it was arrived at.

Worth a short call to see whether it's useful? If the timing's wrong, I'm happy to just send the framework overview so you have it before the next review cycle.
