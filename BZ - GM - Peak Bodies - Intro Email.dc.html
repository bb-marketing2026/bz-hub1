First-touch email to a peak body CEO or Director Sector Development. Peer-to-peer, member-benefit framing, one CTA. Attach the closest case study. Placeholders in [brackets].

Governance done differently
Subject: A governance benchmark for [sector] — and a member benefit
From: Pat Scally <pats@bluezoo.com.au>
To: [First name] [Last name], [Peak body]
Attached: Case study — [NDS / Sport 4 All / Public Sector Commission]

Dear [First name],

[Peak body] speaks for [sector] to government, funders and the public, and is expected to know how the sector is performing. On governance, most peak bodies I work with are working from anecdote — the members who ask for help, and the ones who end up in the media.

There is a way to change that which serves members rather than policing them.

Governance Manager can be deployed across a membership as a governance aggregator. Each member self‑assesses against the frameworks that matter to the sector — or against your own code of practice — and receives their own confidential maturity profile and improvement plan. You see the aggregate, anonymised. That produces three things a peak body rarely has:

  • A sector benchmark you set, publish back to members, and re‑measure annually
  • The common weaknesses across members — where sector development effort will yield the most
  • Identified centres of best practice worth celebrating, and worth learning from

This is well trodden ground. National Disability Services offers it to its ~80 WA members as a value‑added service and gains whole‑of‑sector oversight in return. The Department of Sport and Recreation benchmarked 93 associations to inform the discharge of $4 million in ICT funding. The Public Sector Commission used it to distribute the WA public sector governance framework across 130 agencies. I've attached the closest of those to your situation.

I'd like to offer a free pilot: one framework domain, assessed across up to ten volunteer members, producing an anonymised sector snapshot you can take to your board. No cost, and no obligation to proceed.

One point worth making early: members own their own results. Nothing member‑identified reaches you unless that member chooses to share it. That confidentiality is what makes the membership willing to participate.

Would a 45‑minute conversation be worthwhile, to scope which frameworks and which members? If sector capability funding is something you're pursuing, that is also the natural moment to look at this — we can help build the submission.

Send notes
  • Always attach a case study, and pick the closest sector match — NDS for community services, Sport 4 All for grant‑distributing bodies, Public Sector Commission for regulators, LuSE for licensing or listing bodies.
  • “Serves members rather than policing them” is the sentence that keeps the email alive. A compliance framing gets refused.
  • The confidentiality paragraph is non‑negotiable — it pre‑answers the objection the CEO will otherwise raise on the call.
  • Alternative subjects: “How well is [sector] governed?” · “A sector benchmark, built from member self‑assessment”. Follow up once after 7–10 business days.
