The campaign

Could you defend your fleet decisions to an auditor tomorrow?

Fleet Managers run one of the largest asset bases in the organisation, carry real safety and regulatory exposure, and are usually judged on cost per kilometre. What they rarely have is an evidenced governance position — the thing an auditor, an insurer or a chief executive asks for after an incident.

This campaign offers fleet leadership an objective read on the governance around their fleet, using the FLEETassess Framework in Governance Manager. It is deliberately not a telematics or fleet-management-system pitch. Fleet Managers already have systems producing operational data; what they lack is a defensible measure of how well the fleet is governed — strategically aligned, compliant, managed across the lifecycle, and driven by analysis rather than habit.

Value proposition

“Make the fleet a strategic asset that drives business value — not a cost line you defend each budget cycle.”

The offer

A free assessment against one domain of the FLEETassess Framework — their choice of the four.

The call to action

Assess one domain in under an hour, then a 30‑minute debrief with a Blue Zoo adviser.

Why this lands with Fleet Managers

It speaks to their exposure. Fleet carries safety, chain‑of‑responsibility, environmental and procurement obligations. A Fleet Manager knows an incident review will examine governance, not just maintenance records.

It arms them for the budget conversation. Fleet is perennially asked to cut. A maturity profile and evidenced lifecycle position turns “we need the funding” into a documented case.

It respects their systems. We are not replacing telematics, maintenance or fuel systems. FLEETassess reads the governance wrapped around them, so it complements rather than competes.

It is fast and private. One domain, under an hour, and the result is theirs — nothing goes to the executive unless they choose to send it.

Positioning discipline: never lead with cost savings. Fleet Managers hear that pitch weekly and discount it. Lead with defensibility and strategic standing — the savings follow from better lifecycle and utilisation governance, and land more credibly as a consequence than a claim.

Section one

Ideal customer profile

The best fit is an organisation with a fleet large enough that governance failure is expensive, operating under real regulatory scrutiny, and with a Fleet Manager senior enough to be accountable for lifecycle and procurement decisions.

Attribute
Ideal profile
Sector
Local government, state agencies, utilities, health & ambulance, emergency services, mining & resources, transport & logistics, agriculture, civil construction
Fleet size
50–5,000+ assets; light vehicles, heavy plant, specialist or mixed fleets
Structure
A dedicated fleet function or asset management team; fleet reported through Operations, Infrastructure or Corporate Services
Regulatory context
Chain of Responsibility, WHS duties, NHVR obligations, emissions and environmental reporting, public procurement rules
Trigger events
Fleet replacement programme; EV transition planning; a serious incident or near miss; insurance renewal; an adverse audit finding; new Fleet Manager appointed; outsourcing or in‑sourcing review
Disqualifiers
Fewer than ~30 vehicles; fleet fully novated or leased with no client‑side governance role; no named fleet owner

Buying roles

Primary — the champion

Fleet Manager, Fleet & Facilities Manager, Manager Plant & Fleet, Asset Manager. Owns the problem, feels the exposure, and can authorise a free baseline without procurement.

Economic buyer

Director Operations, Director Infrastructure, Chief Operating Officer, Director Corporate Services. Signs for an ongoing assessment pack once the baseline proves useful.

Influencer — risk & safety

WHS Manager, Risk Manager, Chief Audit Executive, insurer or broker. Often the strongest advocate, because evidenced fleet governance reduces claim and audit exposure.

Blocker to expect

A Fleet Manager who reads assessment as a precursor to outsourcing. Address directly: the baseline is theirs, and a strong governance position is the best defence against being outsourced.

Fleet Managers are practical, sceptical of vendors, and used to being approached about telematics, fuel cards and leasing. The message has to sound like it came from someone who understands the accountability, not the product catalogue.

What keeps them awake
A serious incident and the review that follows it
Replacement decisions made on age, not evidence
Utilisation nobody can actually quantify
Compliance records held in three systems and a spreadsheet
An EV transition with no governance framework behind it
Being asked to cut 10% with no analysis to argue back
What they want instead
A defensible governance position, in writing
Replacement and disposal decisions grounded in lifecycle data
Compliance evidence that exists before it is requested
A seat in the strategic conversation, not just the budget one
A credible basis to argue for or against a funding change
Fewer surprises from the parts of the fleet they see least

Section three

What FLEETassess actually measures

Four governance capabilities, each scored for maturity and for the confidence with which it could be evidenced — so a Fleet Manager can see both how strong a practice is and how well it would stand up to review.

Strategic alignment
Fleet operations are fully aligned with the strategic goals of the organisation, so the fleet supports business efficiency and effectiveness rather than running to its own logic.
Regulatory compliance
Compliance with all applicable regulations is maintained and current — reducing the risk of penalties and lifting safety across fleet operations.
Lifecycle management
Vehicles are governed from procurement to disposal, so performance and cost are managed across the whole operational tenure rather than at replacement time.
Data analytics
Fleet data is harnessed to produce actionable insight, so decisions are driven by analysis rather than habit, anecdote or the age of the asset.

The outcomes we can credibly promise

Reduced operational costs

Better lifecycle management and strategic cost control produce substantial financial savings — as a consequence of governance, not a promise made up front.

Improved compliance and safety

Fleet operations adhere to relevant legal requirements, lifting safety and reducing the likelihood of regulatory issues.

Optimised fleet utilisation

Strategic deployment and maintenance scheduling maximise utilisation, reducing downtime and increasing efficiency.

Informed strategic decisions

Data-driven insight supports decision-making and aligns fleet management with broader business objectives and future growth.

Campaign mechanics

A four‑week sequence per cohort, run by the BDM with Pat Scally on the debriefs. Everything is tracked in HubSpot against the standard deal stages.

Week 1
Reach

Intro email to the cohort; LinkedIn posts 1–3; connection requests to Fleet Managers.

Week 2
Engage

Follow‑up email; posts 4–6; connected calls to book domain assessments. Minimum 20 connected calls.

Week 3
Assess

Domain assessments run; posts 7–9; profiles produced and debriefs scheduled.

Week 4
Convert

30‑minute debriefs; posts 10–12; proposals for ongoing assessment packs.

One cohort per month. Local government fleet networks and the Australasian Fleet Management Association are the fastest routes to a warm list.
