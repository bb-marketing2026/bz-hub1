Post 01 · The hook

When a buyer's adviser interrogates a valuation, they don't start with the answer. They start with the methodology. Which approach, and why. Applied consistently across comparable assets? Who owns the key assumptions, and where are they documented? What independent review did the model get before the number left finance? Most finance leaders are confident their valuations are sound. Post 02 · Consistency

A test worth running inside your own finance function. Ask two people to value comparable assets independently. Then compare the methodology, not just the answer. If they diverge materially, you don't have a valuation problem — you have a standardisation problem, and it will surface at the worst possible moment: in diligence, or in an audit query you can't close quickly. Standardised practice is one of four VALassess domains. Free assessment on that domain alone — comment "standardisation".

Post 03 · Key-person risk

Here's the valuation governance question that makes most CFOs pause. If your most experienced valuation person left next month, would the process survive intact? In a lot of finance functions the methodology lives in one person's judgement rather than in documentation. That isn't a reflection on them — they're usually excellent. It's an exposure that exists precisely because they're excellent, so nobody has needed to write it down. Capability is one of the four VALassess domains. Free assessment on just that one.

Post 04 · Audit vs maturity

An audit sign-off and a governed valuation process are not the same assurance. An audit tests whether this year's outcome is materially correct. It doesn't tell you whether the process would produce a defensible answer again next year, under different market conditions, with different people running it. That's a forward-looking question, and it's what a maturity assessment answers. Both matter. Most finance functions only have the first. Free assessment on one VALassess domain — you pick which.

Post 05 · Assumptions

Every valuation rests on assumptions. Few organisations govern them. Discount rate. Growth trajectory. Terminal value. Useful life. Each one is a judgement, and each one moves the answer materially — yet they're often set by whoever built the model, carried forward year to year, and never formally owned or reviewed. Documented, owned, independently reviewed assumptions are what separate a valuation you can defend from one you can only assert. Free VALassess assessment on one domain. Comment or DM.

Post 06 · ESG in the model

ESG factors move valuations now. In most models they still don't appear. They get acknowledged in the narrative, addressed in the sustainability report, and left out of the numbers — which means the valuation reflects a version of the business that stakeholders no longer price. Integrating the full spectrum of value factors, including ESG, is one of the four VALassess domains. It's also the one most finance functions score lowest on. Free assessment on that domain alone. Post 08 · Diligence discount

Valuation discounts in a transaction are rarely about the number being wrong. They're about the buyer not being able to verify how it was reached. Undocumented assumptions, inconsistent methodology across business units, no independent review — every one of those becomes a risk adjustment, and risk adjustments are priced. The process is worth governing well before anyone starts diligence on it. Free VALassess assessment on one domain of your choosing.

Post 09 · Capability vs evidence

A test that separates confident valuation governance from defensible valuation governance. Take any part of your methodology you're sure about. Now ask: if an auditor or a buyer's adviser asked us to prove this today, what would we hand over? A strong practice with weak evidence isn't good governance yet. It's good intentions waiting to be documented. VALassess scores each domain twice — how well it's embedded, and how readily you could prove it. The gap is usually the most useful finding. Post 10 · Impairment season

Impairment testing is where valuation governance gets examined most often, and prepared for least. The question isn't whether your impairment call is right. It's whether the process behind it — methodology, assumptions, review, documentation — would let you answer an audit query in a day rather than a fortnight. The difference between those two outcomes is almost entirely governance, and it's measurable in under an hour. Free VALassess assessment on one domain, before year-end rather than after.
