Post 02 · Succession

Here's the question that separates a succession list from a succession plan. You can probably name a likely successor for each key management role. Can you name the two or three specific competencies that person would need to develop first? Most organisations can do the first and not the second — which means the plan is a list of names, and nobody is actually being prepared. Succession planning is one of five MANAGERassess domains. Free assessment on that one alone.

Post 03 · Development spend

Leadership development budgets are among the first things cut, and the reason is uncomfortable. Their return is asserted rather than measured. We ran the program, people liked it, attendance was good — none of which tells you whether a competency moved. A baseline before and a reassessment after turns training from an act of faith into an investment with a result. It also makes the budget considerably harder to cut. Free assessment on one MANAGERassess domain. Comment "baseline".

Post 04 · The catalogue problem

Most organisations offer development as a catalogue. Managers browse, self-select what appeals, and attend. It's popular, it's easy to administer, and it has almost no relationship to where capability gaps actually sit. Targeted development starts from assessed need, which means you have to assess first. That's the step most organisations skip — and it's why development effort and development need so often diverge. Free assessment on one MANAGERassess domain of your choosing.

Post 05 · The promotion trap

The most common capability gap in any organisation is created deliberately. We promote the strongest technical performer into management, because that's how progression works — and then provide almost no structured preparation for a role that requires an entirely different competency set. Nobody intends this. It happens because the competencies required were never written down, so nobody noticed they were missing. Competency frameworks is one of five MANAGERassess domains. Post 06 · Not performance management

A clarification worth making, because the assumption is reasonable. Assessing managerial competency governance is not performance management by another route. It doesn't rate individuals. It asks whether the organisation has defined what it expects of managers, developed those competencies deliberately, monitored them, given people real feedback, and prepared successors. Every one of those is an organisational obligation, not an individual failing. Free MANAGERassess assessment on one domain, completed by you.

Post 07 · Engagement results

When engagement results point at line managers, the usual response is more manager training. The more useful question is which competency the results are actually pointing at. Communication? Conflict resolution? Setting direction? Giving feedback? Without a competency framework, "improve our managers" is an instruction nobody can act on precisely — so the response is generic, and next year's results look similar. Post 08 · Early warning

How would you find out that a manager has a significant competency gap? In most organisations: through a team problem. Turnover in their area, a grievance, a project that slipped, an engagement score. By that point the gap has already cost something, and the conversation is remedial rather than developmental. Continuous monitoring against defined competencies surfaces the same information earlier, when it's still a development conversation. Free MANAGERassess assessment on one domain.

Post 09 · Real feedback

Ask a manager in your organisation what they're working on developing. If the answer is vague, it isn't because they lack ambition. It's usually because nobody has ever given them a structured read on their own capability — just informal encouragement and the occasional difficult conversation. Structured feedback is one of the five MANAGERassess domains, and one of the cheapest to improve. Free assessment on one domain, completed by you in under an hour.

Post 10 · Consistency

In most organisations, the quality of your working life depends heavily on which manager you happen to report to. That variation isn't a reflection on the individuals. It's what happens when expectations of managers are held informally rather than defined — every manager builds their own version of the role, and the organisation gets as many management styles as it has managers. A defined framework is what makes management quality consistent. Free MANAGERassess assessment on one domain.
