Post 01 · The hook

If a minister asked your peak body how well the sector is governed, what would you show them? Most peak bodies would answer from anecdote — the members who ask for help, and the ones who end up in the media. Neither is a picture of the sector. That gap isn't a failure of effort. It's that nobody has ever aggregated the data, because there was no mechanism to. Free sector pilot: one framework domain, up to ten volunteer members, an anonymised snapshot for your board.

Post 02 · Not policing

The instinctive objection to sector-wide governance assessment is the right one: we're a member body, not a regulator. Which is exactly why it works the way it does. Each member self-assesses and owns their own result — their profile, their improvement plan, confidential to them. The peak body sees the aggregate, anonymised. Nobody is audited by head office. National Disability Services offers precisely this to its ~80 WA members as a value-added service. Free sector pilot available.

Post 03 · Pockets of excellence

Here's the finding that surprises peak bodies most. The capability their sector lacks usually already exists — inside two or three member organisations nobody had identified. Connecting the strong to the struggling is the cheapest sector improvement available. It costs nothing but an introduction and a case study. And it's completely invisible without measurement, because excellence rarely announces itself. Free sector pilot: one domain, ten members, an anonymised snapshot.

#SectorDevelopment #PeakBodies #BestPractice #Governance

Post 04 · Contagion

A significant governance failure at one member damages the standing of every other member. Every peak body knows this. Very few have any early-warning mechanism — the first notice usually arrives with a journalist's phone call. Aggregate governance data won't prevent every failure. But it does show you which domains are weak across the membership, which is where the next failure is most likely to come from. Free sector pilot for peak bodies. Comment or DM.

#SectorReputation #RiskManagement #PeakBodies #Governance

Post 05 · Advocacy with evidence

Peak bodies spend a great deal of energy advocating — for funding, against a regulatory change, for the sector's standing. Almost all of it is argued from assertion about member capability, because that's all that's available. An evidenced sector benchmark changes the register of that conversation entirely. It also lets you demonstrate improvement over time, which is what funders actually want to see before they fund again. Free sector pilot: one domain, ten volunteer members.

Post 06 · Sport 4 All

A worked example of what sector-wide assessment makes possible. The WA Department of Sport and Recreation benchmarked the ICT governance capability of all 93 state sporting associations and peak bodies — the organisations overseeing roughly 5,000 clubs. That unparalleled view of the sector was then used to inform the discharge of $4 million in ICT funding. Not allocated on submissions and relationships. Allocated on measured capability. Free sector pilot available for peak bodies.

Post 07 · Multiple frameworks

Peak bodies rarely have one governance concern. It's corporate governance, and risk, and policy currency, and ICT and cyber, and increasingly remuneration — each of which would traditionally be a separate exercise nobody has the capacity to run. Assessed through one platform, across one membership, they become a single program with one member ask. The sector view covers every domain rather than the one you could afford to look at. Free sector pilot: start with the domain that worries you most.

Post 08 · Your own code

If your peak body publishes a code of practice or governance guideline, here's the uncomfortable question. How do you know members are following it? A code that cannot be measured is a statement of intent. The Lusaka Stock Exchange faced exactly this — so it deployed its own governance code as the assessment framework, benchmarked every listed entity against it, and eventually published a governance index for investors. Your code can be the framework. Free sector pilot.

#CodeOfPractice #Governance #PeakBodies #Standards

Post 09 · Member value

Every peak body is asked the same question at renewal time: what do we get for the fee? Networking, advocacy and information are the usual answers, and they're all true — and all hard to price. A confidential governance maturity assessment, with a benchmark against comparable members and a prioritised improvement plan, is a service a member CEO can immediately value. It's also something they'd struggle to buy alone. Free pilot for peak bodies: one domain, ten members.

Post 11 · The second measurement

The first sector benchmark is interesting. The second one is where the value is. A single assessment gives you a snapshot. Re-running it a year later tells you whether your sector development effort actually moved anything — which domains improved, which stayed flat, and whether the members you targeted are the ones who lifted. Very few peak bodies can currently demonstrate sector improvement. That's a fixable position. Free sector pilot to start the baseline.
