Reading the signals of governance capability

How executives can spot genuine capability in the papers, reports and responses that already cross their desk — no new reporting required.

By Pat Scally · Chief Executive Officer, Blue Zoo

Capability leaves no tidy record the way compliance does. It shows up instead in the texture of everyday governance — in how a decision is argued, how a risk is raised, how a finding is closed. The skill is not collecting new data; it is learning to read what you already receive. Four lenses reveal most of it.

Four lenses · where capability shows up
1 · Board & executive papers

Do papers show options, trade-offs and the basis for a recommendation — or just confirm a template was followed?

2 · Risk & assurance reports

Is risk discussed before events and acted upon — or updated on a cycle and consulted only afterwards?

3 · Performance reporting

Does reporting headline outcomes that changed for the public — or activity that was merely completed?

4 · Culture & recovery

Do people surface problems early, and does the agency recover quickly when something goes wrong?

The capability signal — a spectrum, not a switch
Compliant onlyEmergingGenuinely capable

Read each lens on this spectrum rather than as pass/fail. A single strong paper is not proof; a consistent pattern across all four lenses is. The checklist overleaf turns each lens into a short, honest test.

The reading checklist

Four short tests you can run this week

Take your three most recent board papers, your current risk report, a performance update and your open-findings register. Tick honestly. Mostly ticks in a lens is a capability signal; mostly blanks is a prompt to look closer.

Board & executive papers
✓Options and trade-offs are set out, not just a single recommendation
✓The evidence behind the recommendation is visible
✓Risks of the preferred option are stated plainly
✓A decision to stop or deprioritise something appears at least sometimes
Risk & assurance
✓Leadership can name a risk it acted on before it materialised
✓Audit findings are fewer, less severe and less repeated year on year
✓Assurance effort is concentrated where exposure is greatest
✓Problems are surfaced by staff, not first found by auditors
Performance & outcomes
✓Reports lead with an outcome that changed, not activity completed
✓Measures connect spending to public benefit
✓Staff two levels down can link their work to purpose
✓Improvement continues after a program formally ends
Culture & recovery
✓Bad news reaches the executive quickly, and is rewarded not punished
✓The agency recovers from disruption faster than it used to
✓Lessons from incidents are captured and applied
✓People describe what their work is for, not just what they do
Signal vs noise
Noise: a dashboard of green indicators
Signal: a finding closed at its root cause
Noise: "all milestones met"
Signal: a wait time that measurably fell
