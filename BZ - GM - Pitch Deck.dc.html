The problem with governance today

The status quo

Governance is managed manually, one framework and one snapshot at a time

Complex & slow

Reporting on governance and compliance is time-consuming, and often lives in spreadsheets.

Check-box thinking

Compliance is treated as a baseline to tick, not a level of maturity to build toward.

Point-in-time

Assessed once and filed — rarely part of a continuous improvement cycle.

The cost

A blind spot at the top of the organisation

5

executives, five different answers to "how well are we governed?"

Boards receive abundant assurance that obligations were met — and almost nothing on whether the organisation is becoming more capable. Governance stays a matter of opinion, confident in some rooms and anxious in others, and rarely comparable from one year to the next.

Case study · Peak body

National Disability Services (WA)

A member self-assessment for ~80 disability providers — with whole-of-sector governance reporting for the peak body.

The challenge

Sector governance is under intense scrutiny — one provider's failing can carry across the whole sector.

The approach

A web self-assessment across boards, finance, ethics, people, risk and communications, with tracked agreed actions.

The benefit

Whole-of-sector oversight of governance strengths and weaknesses — and better-governed, more transparent providers.

Case study · State government

Sport4All — Department of Sport & Recreation

Benchmarking ICT capability maturity across the sector to direct a multi-year, multi-million-dollar grant program.

93

$20m

four-year grant program the data helped direct

A custom small-business ICT framework scored each club on a level 1–4 maturity scale — assessing, comparing and improving ICT capacity across the sector to inform where funding would do the most good.

The point isn't to reach level 4 everywhere — it's finding the right level for each organisation's size and needs.
