The campaign

Could you explain why two people doing similar work are paid differently?

HR and People leaders own the most scrutinised numbers in the organisation after the financial statements. Pay decisions get examined by employees, unions, tribunals, auditors and increasingly by public reporting obligations — and the question is almost never “is this number right?” but “on what basis was it set?”

This campaign offers HR and People & Culture leadership an objective read on remuneration governance using the REMassess Framework in Governance Manager. It is deliberately not a salary benchmarking or payroll pitch. These leaders already buy market data; what they lack is a defensible measure of the governance around pay — whether the framework is structured, compliant, performance‑linked, internally equitable and analysed.

Value proposition

“Understand and manage pay structures effectively — with transparency and internal equity you can evidence.”

The offer

A free assessment against one domain of the REMassess Framework — their choice of the five.

The call to action

Assess one domain in under an hour, then a 30‑minute confidential debrief with a Blue Zoo adviser.

Why this lands with HR and People leaders

The exposure is personal and current. Pay equity reporting, underpayment enforcement and wage compliance have moved remuneration from an HR process to a governance risk with named accountability.

It arms them for the board. HR leaders are regularly asked to justify the pay framework to a remuneration committee with little more than market data. A maturity position changes that conversation.

It is discreet. Remuneration is the most sensitive data in the organisation. One domain, under an hour, no pay data required, and the result stays with them.

One domain is an easy yes. A whole‑framework review reads as a project. One domain, chosen by them, reads as an hour they can find this week.

Positioning discipline: never imply the organisation underpays, discriminates or has an equity problem. That reads as an accusation and will end the conversation instantly. The premise is that pay decisions are made in good faith and the governance around them is unmeasured — which is a documentation gap, not an ethics one.

Section one

Ideal customer profile

The best fit is an organisation large enough for pay inconsistency to have crept in, with real compliance obligations around remuneration, and an HR or People leader accountable for the framework rather than only for payroll delivery.

Attribute
Ideal profile
Sector
Local government, state agencies and statutory authorities, health and aged care, education, utilities, not‑for‑profits, member associations, mid‑market private companies, ASX small and mid‑cap
Size
150–5,000 employees. Large enough for pay drift and multiple agreements, small enough that a global reward consultancy is not already retained
Structure
An HR or People & Culture function with a named leader; ideally a remuneration or people committee of the board, or an executive that reviews the pay framework annually
Compliance context
Awards and enterprise agreements, Fair Work obligations, WGEA gender pay gap reporting, public‑sector wages policy, SAT determinations, superannuation and payroll tax exposure
Trigger events
Enterprise agreement negotiation; a new HR or People leader; WGEA reporting or a published pay gap; an underpayment or classification issue; annual remuneration review; a restructure creating inconsistent classifications; retention problems in a critical role
Disqualifiers
Fewer than ~100 employees; pay set entirely by award with no discretion; a reward consultancy engagement already underway

Buying roles

Primary — the champion

HR Director or Manager, Director or Manager People & Culture, Chief People Officer. Owns the framework, carries the exposure, and can authorise a free single‑domain assessment without procurement.

Secondary champion

Remuneration & Benefits Manager, Manager Workforce or People Services. Often runs the framework in practice and feels the inconsistency most acutely.

Influencer

Remuneration committee chair, CFO, Chief Audit Executive. The CFO in particular cares about remuneration cost governance and is often an easier first conversation than HR.

Blocker to expect

An HR leader who reads any external interest in pay as an audit or a legal risk. Lead with confidentiality and the fact that no pay data is required.

Section two

The HR leader, in their own terms

HR and People leaders are relationship‑driven, heavily prospected by reward vendors, and acutely conscious that remuneration is the most sensitive topic they hold. They respond to discretion, to peer credibility, and to anything that reduces the risk of a pay conversation going badly.

What keeps them awake
Two people doing similar work on materially different pay
A classification decision nobody can explain years later
A published pay gap they have to account for
Bonus outcomes with no documented link to performance
An underpayment discovered in a payroll review
Losing a critical role and suspecting pay was the reason
What they want instead
A structured framework applied consistently
Every pay decision traceable to a documented basis
Internal equity they can evidence, not just assert
A defensible answer for the remuneration committee
Performance genuinely linked to reward
Analytics that show where pay is drifting, early

Section three

What REMassess actually measures

Five governance domains, each scored for maturity and for the confidence with which it could be evidenced — so an HR leader can see both how strong a practice is and how well it would stand up to scrutiny. The free offer covers one domain of their choosing.

Structured frameworks
A structured remuneration framework applied consistently and transparently, with compensation strategy aligned to business objectives rather than set case by case.
Compliance management
Compliance with all relevant remuneration regulation is current and tracked, so practices adhere to the latest legal requirements rather than last year's understanding.
Performance‑linked pay
Performance management is genuinely integrated with compensation — metrics set, tracked and demonstrably influencing reward, so pay rewards actual performance.
Equity & benchmarking
Internal equity is analysed and external benchmarking performed regularly, supporting fair pay across the organisation and competitiveness in the market.
Analytics & reporting
Remuneration policy effectiveness is evaluated with analytics that identify trends and disparities needing attention — rather than reporting that only confirms spend.

The outcomes we can credibly promise

Enhanced strategic alignment

Remuneration strategy tied to business goals, with incentive structures that help achieve them and employees who understand how their effort contributes.

Improved compliance & risk

Reduced risk of non‑compliance with remuneration regulation, supporting corporate reputation and employee trust.

Greater equity & satisfaction

Fair, transparent pay practices improve morale, reduce disputes and are associated with lower turnover and higher engagement.

Data‑driven decisions

Analytics that support informed compensation decisions and more effective budgeting of remuneration cost.

A fifth outcome — streamlined remuneration processes — follows from standardisation: fewer administrative errors, and faster adjustment when strategy changes.

Campaign mechanics

A four‑week sequence per cohort, run by the BDM with Pat Scally on the debriefs. Everything is tracked in HubSpot against the standard deal stages.

Week 1
Reach

Intro email to the cohort; LinkedIn posts 1–3; connection requests to HR and People & Culture leaders.

Week 2
Engage

Follow‑up email; posts 4–6; connected calls to book domain assessments. Minimum 20 connected calls.

Week 3
Assess

Domain assessments run; posts 7–9; profiles produced and confidential debriefs scheduled.

Week 4
Convert

30‑minute debriefs; posts 10–12; proposals for the remaining domains or an ongoing assessment pack.

One cohort per month. Time cohorts ahead of the annual remuneration review and WGEA reporting deadlines — that is when an evidenced position is worth most. AHRI and local government HR networks are the fastest routes to a warm list.
