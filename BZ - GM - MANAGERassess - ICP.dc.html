The campaign

You know who your best managers are. Could you prove it, or explain why?

Every executive can name their strongest and weakest managers. What almost no organisation can produce is a defined statement of the competencies a manager at each level is expected to hold, an assessment against it, and evidence that development spend is closing the gaps that matter.

This campaign offers executives and People & Culture leadership an objective read on managerial competency using the MANAGERassess Framework in Governance Manager. It is deliberately not a leadership‑training pitch. These organisations already buy training; what they lack is the competency framework and measurement that would tell them whether any of it worked.

Value proposition

“Empower managers and build smart leaders — with a competency baseline that shows where development actually needs to go.”

The offer

A free assessment against one domain of the MANAGERassess Framework — their choice of the five.

The call to action

Assess one domain in under an hour, then a 30‑minute debrief with a Blue Zoo adviser.

Why this lands with executives and People leaders

Management capability is the felt constraint. Most executives will privately say their strategy is fine and their middle management is the limiting factor. This campaign speaks directly to that, without blaming anyone.

It makes training defensible. Development budgets are among the first cut, because their return is asserted rather than measured. A competency baseline changes that conversation.

Succession is a live board question. Boards increasingly ask who is ready to step up. Most organisations answer from opinion; a competency assessment answers from evidence.

One domain is an easy yes. Assessing all managers across a whole framework reads as a project. One domain, chosen by them, reads as an hour.

Positioning discipline: never suggest the managers are underperforming. Framed as a judgement on individuals, this becomes politically impossible for an executive to introduce. Framed as measuring whether the organisation has defined and supported what it expects of managers, it becomes something they can champion.

Section one

Ideal customer profile

The best fit is an organisation with enough management layers for capability to vary widely, a development budget already being spent, and an executive or People leader who is being asked about succession.

Attribute
Ideal profile
Sector
Local government, state agencies and statutory authorities, health and aged care, education, utilities, resources, not‑for‑profits, mid‑market private companies
Size
200–5,000 employees with 20–300 people in management roles across two or more layers — enough for capability to vary materially
Structure
A People & Culture or HR function with a learning and development capability; an executive team that reviews talent or succession at least annually
Spend context
An existing leadership development or training budget, typically with no measurement of competency movement attached to it
Trigger events
A board or executive question about succession; a new CEO or People leader; an engagement survey showing manager‑related concerns; turnover in a critical management layer; a restructure creating first‑time managers; a leadership program being renewed or tendered
Disqualifiers
Fewer than ~15 managers; no development budget at all; a competency framework project already underway with an external firm

Buying roles

Primary — the champion

Director or Manager People & Culture, HR Director, Chief People Officer. Owns capability development and can authorise a free single‑domain assessment without procurement.

Economic buyer

Chief Executive, Chief Operating Officer, Director General or Executive Director. Often the more receptive first conversation, because management capability is their felt constraint.

Influencer

Learning & Development Manager, Organisational Development Manager. Usually delighted by anything that lets them target spend and prove effect.

Blocker to expect

A People leader who reads competency assessment as performance management by another name. Address it directly: this assesses the framework and the organisation's support of it, not individuals' ratings.

Section two

The executive and People leader, in their own terms

Executives think in terms of delivery constraints and succession risk. People leaders think in terms of capability, engagement and the credibility of their development spend. The message has to serve both, because the champion and the buyer are usually different people.

What keeps them awake
A board asking who is ready to step up, with no real answer
Management capability varying wildly between teams
Training spend with no measurable effect on capability
Strong technical people promoted into management unprepared
Engagement results that point squarely at line managers
Losing a capable manager and having no successor
What they want instead
A defined statement of what a manager at each level must be able to do
A measured view of where capability actually sits
Development targeted at the gaps that matter most
Evidence that last year's investment moved something
A succession answer grounded in assessment, not opinion
Consistent management quality across the organisation

Objections and honest answers

Objection
Response
“We already run a leadership program.”
Good — and MANAGERassess tells you whether it's aimed at the right competencies and whether they moved. It makes the program measurable rather than replacing it.
“Is this performance management?”
No. It assesses whether the organisation has defined the competencies it expects, developed them deliberately, and can evidence the result. It isn't a rating tool for individuals.
“We have a capability framework already.”
Many organisations do — the useful question is whether anyone is assessed against it, and whether development is tied to the gaps it identifies. That's what gets measured here.
“Our managers won't have time.”
The single‑domain assessment is completed by you, not by every manager. Under an hour, and it tells you where a broader assessment would be worth running.
“What does it cost afterwards?”
MANAGERassess starts from $576 for assessment packs, scaling by number of assessments and access period. It is the most accessible entry point in the Governance Manager range.

Section three

What MANAGERassess actually measures

Five governance domains, each scored for maturity and for the confidence with which it could be evidenced — so a leader can see both how strong a practice is and how well it would stand up to scrutiny. The free offer covers one domain of their choosing.

Competency frameworks
A structured framework defining what competencies are needed at each managerial level, standardised across the organisation so management quality is consistent.
Development programs
Development is customised to assessed need, so training interventions are targeted and managers receive learning that enhances their specific competencies.
Performance monitoring
Managerial performance is monitored continuously against the defined competencies, so gaps are identified early and interventions are timely.
Feedback mechanisms
Structured feedback gives managers insight into their competency levels, supporting personal development and a culture of self‑improvement and accountability.
Succession planning
Potential leaders and their competency gaps are identified, preparing the next line of management and protecting continuity of leadership.

The outcomes we can credibly promise

Enhanced managerial effectiveness

Improved competencies lead to better decisions and increased team productivity, contributing directly to strategic objectives.

Reduced turnover, higher engagement

Managers who feel supported and challenged stay longer — and engaged managers cultivate engaged teams.

A leadership pipeline

Identifying and developing potential leaders ensures a steady supply of capable managers for long‑term planning.

Reduced competency gaps

Tailored development closes specific gaps, producing more consistent and competent management across the board.

Two further outcomes follow: greater agility in management practice as managers adapt to change, and strategic alignment — development aimed at future organisational needs, not only current ones.

Section four

Campaign mechanics

A four‑week sequence per cohort, run by the BDM with Pat Scally on the debriefs. Because the champion and the economic buyer differ here, run two parallel lists — People leaders and executives — with the same offer.

Week 1
Reach

Intro email to both lists; LinkedIn posts 1–3; connection requests to executives and People leaders.

Week 2
Engage

Follow‑up email; posts 4–6; connected calls to book domain assessments. Minimum 20 connected calls.

Week 3
Assess

Domain assessments run; posts 7–9; profiles produced and debriefs scheduled.

Week 4
Convert

30‑minute debriefs; posts 10–12; proposals for a full manager cohort assessment.

One cohort per month. Time cohorts to the annual planning and development‑budget cycle, and immediately after engagement survey results are released — that is when manager capability is most on the executive's mind. AHRI and AICD networks are the fastest routes to a warm list.
