Travel Policy

How Blue Zoo people plan, approve and undertake travel on company business — so every journey is considered, safe and defensible.

This policy sets out Blue Zoo's requirements for planning and undertaking travel on company business. It applies to all employees and contractors, whether travelling domestically or internationally. Its purpose is to keep our people safe, to make travel spending considered and transparent, and to ensure that every trip has a clear plan that a line manager has seen and approved before it begins.

1. Purpose and scope

Blue Zoo advises boards and executive teams, and our people travel to be alongside clients. This policy exists so that travel is planned properly, undertaken safely, and represents a considered use of company funds. It covers all travel undertaken for Blue Zoo business, including client visits, conferences, training and off-site work, for both employees and contractors.

It does not cover an individual's ordinary daily commute to their usual place of work.

3. Policy statement

Any employee or contractor travelling on company business must submit a Journey Plan to their line manager for approval at least one week (seven calendar days) before the intended departure date. Travel must not be booked or undertaken until the Journey Plan has been approved.

Where travel is genuinely urgent and seven days' notice is not possible, the traveller must contact their line manager as early as practicable and obtain approval before any commitment is made. Approval of a short-notice Journey Plan is at the line manager's discretion.

4. The Journey Plan

The Journey Plan is a single document that describes the whole trip. It must be complete before it is submitted, and it must cover each of the following.

4.1 Travel itinerary and budget

Set out the proposed arrangements and their estimated cost so the manager can approve both the plan and the spend together.

  • Flights — proposed carrier, dates, times, route and fare class.
  • Accommodation — property, location, nightly rate and number of nights.
  • Land transport — airport transfers, hire car, rideshare, rail or taxi as required at each location.
  • Budget — an itemised estimate covering flights, accommodation, land transport, meals and incidentals, with a total for the trip.

4.3 Risk assessment

Provide a basic assessment of the risks associated with the trip and the steps to manage them. As a minimum, consider:

  • Destination safety, including any government travel advisories for the location.
  • Health considerations — vaccinations, medical facilities, medications and any personal health needs.
  • Transport and travel-timing risks, including late-night arrivals and unfamiliar routes.
  • Working alone or after hours, and how the traveller will stay in contact.
  • Any client-site or activity-specific risks, and the controls to be put in place.

4.4 Emergency contact details

Confirm and, where necessary, update the emergency contact details held for the traveller. The Journey Plan must record:

  • The traveller's nominated next of kin or emergency contact and their phone number.
  • The traveller's own mobile number that will be reachable during travel.
  • For international travel, relevant local emergency numbers and the nearest embassy or consulate.

5. Approval and authorisation

Travel and its budget are authorised together through the Journey Plan. The required approver depends on the type of trip and its total cost.

Trip typeMinimum approver
Domestic, within budget guidanceLine manager
Domestic, above budget guidanceGeneral Manager
International travelGeneral Manager
Travel to a high-risk destinationChief Operating Officer

The approval process is:

  1. The traveller completes the Journey Plan and submits it to their line manager at least seven days before departure.
  2. The line manager reviews the itinerary, budget, meetings, risk assessment and emergency contacts, and escalates to a higher approver where the table above requires it.
  3. The approver approves, requests changes, or declines the plan. Approval is confirmed in writing (email is sufficient).
  4. Only once the plan is approved may the traveller book flights, accommodation and transport.
  5. The approved Journey Plan is retained by the line manager and a copy is provided to the office for duty-of-care records.

7. Travel bookings and standards

Bookings should be made to reasonable standards of cost and comfort, and travellers must use Blue Zoo's nominated travel agent or booking channel and preferred airline, hotel and car-hire suppliers wherever practical. Flights should be booked as far in advance as possible — and at least 14 days ahead for domestic and 21 days ahead for international travel — to secure fares, unless the trip is genuinely short-notice.

8. Expenses, allowances and payment

8.1 Allowances and caps

  • Meals and incidentals — reimbursed against receipts or paid as a per diem, up to the daily allowance set for the destination.
  • Accommodation — booked within the nightly cap for the city; travellers should seek approval before exceeding it.
  • Allowance and cap schedules are maintained by Operations and reviewed annually.

The following schedules apply to travel within Australian capital cities. Accommodation caps are the maximum nightly rate (room only, excluding GST where separately itemised) for a standard CBD three-star hotel. The per diem is the total daily meal allowance, made up of the breakfast, lunch and dinner components shown; for part-days, claim only the meals actually taken.

8.2 Payment, cards and currency

Where issued, a corporate card should be used for travel costs; otherwise reasonable costs are met by the traveller and reimbursed. Foreign-currency spend is reimbursed at the card's converted rate or the prevailing rate on the transaction date. Receipts (tax invoices) are required for all claims above $25, and expense claims must be submitted within 14 days of returning in line with the Expense Policy.

8.3 Personal and non-reimbursable expenses

The following are not reimbursed: minibar, in-room entertainment, personal phone calls, laundry on short trips, traffic or parking fines, travel companions' costs, and any portion of a trip that is personal. Frequent-flyer points and loyalty benefits earned on Blue Zoo travel may be retained by the traveller for personal use, provided they never influence the choice of the most economical booking.

9. Travel insurance, health and medical

Travellers on approved trips are covered by Blue Zoo's corporate travel insurance, including medical and emergency evacuation cover; travel undertaken without an approved Journey Plan may not be covered. Before international travel, travellers must confirm required vaccinations and carry any personal medications with supporting documentation. Any pre-existing condition relevant to cover should be disclosed to Operations in confidence. Insurer contact details and the policy number are provided with the approved Journey Plan, and claims are made through Operations.

10. Safety, security and duty of care

Blue Zoo takes its duty of care to travelling staff and contractors seriously. The Journey Plan is the primary record used to locate and support a traveller if something goes wrong.

  • Check-in and tracking — travellers keep their itinerary current with Operations and check in on arrival and on any major change of location.
  • Emergency support — a 24/7 emergency contact is available; the number is issued with the approved Journey Plan.
  • High-risk destinations — travel to a location under a "Reconsider" or "Do not travel" advisory requires COO approval and an enhanced risk assessment.
  • Lone and after-hours travel — travellers avoid unnecessary lone travel at night, share arrival times, and stay reachable.
  • Incident and near-miss reporting — any incident, illness, security concern or near miss is reported to the line manager and Operations as soon as practicable.
  • Data security — use company devices with encryption and the VPN, avoid untrusted public Wi-Fi for company data, and never leave devices or documents unattended.

11. Combining business and personal travel

Travellers may extend a business trip with personal leave (bleisure) with prior manager approval. Blue Zoo pays only the costs it would have incurred for the business portion; all additional flights, accommodation, transport and activities for the personal portion are at the traveller's own expense, and insurance cover applies only to the business days unless the traveller arranges their own cover.

12. Environmental responsibility

Consider whether travel is necessary or whether a video meeting would achieve the same outcome. Where travel is warranted, favour rail over short-haul flights where practical, choose direct routes, and combine trips to reduce the number of journeys. Blue Zoo tracks travel emissions and supports offsetting in line with its sustainability commitments.

13. Accessibility and reasonable adjustments

Blue Zoo makes reasonable adjustments so that travellers with disability, medical needs or other requirements can travel safely and comfortably. Travellers should raise any needs — accessible accommodation, seating, equipment or additional support — with their manager and Operations during planning so arrangements can be made in advance and at company cost where appropriate.

14. Non-compliance

Travel undertaken without an approved Journey Plan is a breach of this policy. It may result in expenses not being reimbursed, insurance cover being unavailable, and, for repeated or serious breaches, disciplinary action. Contractors who do not comply may have their engagement reviewed.

15. Review and related documents

This policy is reviewed every twelve months, or sooner if travel patterns, insurance arrangements or legal obligations change. Questions about the policy should be directed to the Chief Operating Officer. This policy should be read together with the Expense Policy, the Work Health & Safety Policy, the Code of Conduct and the Information Security Policy.
