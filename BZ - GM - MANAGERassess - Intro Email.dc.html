Most executives I speak with can name their strongest and weakest managers without hesitation. Very few organisations can produce a defined statement of what a manager at each level is expected to be able to do, an assessment against it, and evidence that development spending is closing the gaps that matter.

That gap shows up in two places. The first is the succession question — when a board asks who is ready to step up, the answer is usually formed from opinion. The second is the development budget, which is asserted to be valuable and is therefore among the first things cut.

The MANAGERassess Framework in Governance Manager measures managerial competency governance across five domains: structured competency frameworks, customised development programs, performance monitoring, feedback mechanisms and succession planning. Each is scored for maturity and for how readily it could be evidenced.

Worth being clear about what it is not: this doesn't rate individual managers, and it isn't performance management by another route. It measures whether the organisation has defined what it expects of managers and supported them to get there.

I'd like to offer you a free assessment against one domain of the framework — whichever you choose. Practically:

  • Under an hour of your time, completed by you — not by every manager
  • A maturity profile on that domain and a prioritised gap list, in writing
  • A 30‑minute debrief with me to talk through what it shows
  • The result is yours — nothing is shared unless you send it

The domain most organisations find most revealing is succession planning, but pick whichever you're least comfortable about.

Worth a short call to see whether it's useful? If the timing's wrong, I'm happy to just send the framework overview so you have it before the next planning cycle.

Send notes
  • The “what it is not” paragraph is essential — without it, People leaders read this as performance management and executives read it as a judgement on their team. Do not cut it.
  • Run two lists with the same email: executives (CEO/COO/DG) and People leaders. The executive list usually replies faster; the People leader is the one who runs it.
  • Alternative subjects: “Management capability, measured” · “Does your development spend move anything?”
  • “Completed by you, not by every manager” removes the biggest practical objection. One CTA only; follow up once after 4–5 business days.
