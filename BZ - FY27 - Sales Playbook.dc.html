The operating reality

Selling with 0.5 FTE

Blue Zoo has around half of the Managing Director's time available for sales — senior, expensive and finite. The whole model is built to protect that time for the few activities only the MD can do, and to hand everything else to tools and process.

0.5 FTE

MD time for sales

2 / wk

proactive sales meetings

6 / mth

new business meetings

>$200K

adjusted pipeline < 30 days

Protect the MD's time for
  • Discovery and scoping conversations with executives.
  • Relationship building with senior sponsors and panels.
  • Shaping and presenting proposals; negotiation and close.
  • Key-account expansion and referrals.
Delegate or automate
  • Lead capture, reminders and follow-ups — HubSpot.
  • First-draft proposal writing — Claude Design.
  • Proposal assembly, sending & e-sign — PandaDoc.
  • Content & campaigns — the Marketing Playbook.

The principle: proactive, not reactive. With so little selling time, we pursue fewer, better-qualified opportunities, lead with the problems agencies already have, and let tools carry the administrative and drafting load.

Who we sell to

Ideal client profile & segments

Focus is the whole game at 0.5 FTE. We sell where our track record, panels and platform give us an edge — the Western Australian public sector.

Target segments
  • WA state government agencies
  • Local government
  • Statutory authorities
  • Boards & audit / risk committees
Buyers & sponsors

  • Directors, Corporate Services / CFO

  • AI adoption without governance in place

  • Timeline with no real trigger or decision date

Choosing where to spend the time

Qualifying what to pursue

With 0.5 FTE, the fastest way to win more is to say no faster. Every opportunity is scored against six tests before it earns MD time. Qualify hard, and re-qualify at each stage.

TestAskPursue when…
ProblemIs there a real, funded problem we solve — audit, AI governance, capability?A named pain with consequences.
SponsorIs there an executive who owns the outcome and will champion us?DG, Director or ARC-level sponsor.
FundingIs there budget and a procurement route (panel / CUA / direct)?Money and a path to contract.
TimingIs there a trigger and a decision date within a useful horizon?A real deadline, not "someday".
FitDoes it sit in our governance / strategy / risk sweet spot?Plays to our track record & GM.
MarginWill it clear our 25% gross-profit floor at our rates?Profitable at rate card.
Pursue

5–6 tests met. Advance in HubSpot and invest MD time.

Park / nurture

3–4 tests. Keep warm with content; no MD time yet.

Decline

0–2 tests. Politely decline and free the time.

Free assessments (Performance Audit Readiness, AI Governance Benchmark, Governance Health Check) are our best qualification tool — a low-cost first step that surfaces the problem, the sponsor and the funding before a full proposal.

How work moves

The pipeline stages (HubSpot)

One deal pipeline in HubSpot, eight stages, each with a clear exit test and a probability weighting used for the adjusted pipeline forecast.

#StageExit criteria — advance only when…Weight
1ContactedFit account and buyer identified; first outreach made.10%
2InterestedProspect has engaged and expressed genuine interest.20%
3Meeting scheduledA discovery meeting is booked in the calendar.30%
4Meeting heldDiscovery done; problem, sponsor, funding and timing confirmed.45%
5Request receivedClient has asked for a proposal or quote.60%
6Proposal sentScoped proposal issued via PandaDoc and acknowledged.75%
7NegotiationVerbal yes; terms, scope and price being finalised.90%
8Closed won / lostSigned in PandaDoc and handed to delivery — or closed lost with a reason.100% / 0%
Pipeline hygiene rules
  • Every deal has a next step and a date, always.
  • No deal sits in a stage > 30 days without action.
  • Close lost honestly — a clean pipeline forecasts better.
Adjusted pipeline

Deal value × stage weight = adjusted value. The sum of deals expected to close within 30 days is the number we hold above $200K at all times, and report to the Board monthly.

Making 0.5 FTE count

The weekly sales rhythm

A fixed cadence turns limited, senior time into consistent output. Sales works best in the morning, so the sales day starts at 8:30am with a structured hour before meetings and delivery take over.

15 hrs

Minimum sales hours recorded every week — non-negotiable. The first hour of each day is preserved for HubSpot and prospecting; the remaining sales time is within the Managing Director's locus of control.

The 8:30am start — first hour, every day
8:30 – 8:45 · HubSpot
15 min

Update the CRM so the pipeline is always clean:

  • No deals missing information
  • No deals with > 14 days inactivity
  • No deals missing a Next Step
8:45 – 9:30 · Prospecting
45 min

Straight into active outreach:

  • Reach-outs and follow-up emails & calls
  • Trying to schedule meetings
  • Focus on NfN and NfE — no EfE
CadenceActivity
Daily (from 8:30am)15 min HubSpot hygiene, then 45 min prospecting; respond to inbound within 24 hours; mornings protected for sales.
WeeklyTwo sales / discovery meetings; 15-minute pipeline review; send any proposals due.
Twice weeklyReview panel bid requests (CUA / WALGA); triage which to pursue against the six qualification tests.
MonthlySix new business-development meetings; supply four content pieces to marketing; pipeline & forecast to the Board.
QuarterlyKey-account review and expansion planning; refresh target-account list; campaign alignment with marketing.

Campaign sync: Tuesday 9:30–10:00, a fast meeting with Sean on campaign updates and input on progress. Pat commits to > 20 'connected calls' from each campaign, irrespective of inbound levels — tracked in HubSpot.

Panel bids: review requests twice weekly; cap preparation at a maximum 2 hours from decision to lodgment — tracked in Xero timesheets.

Responding to RFQs is not sales. It is document building — a different skill. RFQ / bid preparation is tracked separately (in Xero timesheets) and must never be counted toward, or allowed to displace, the Managing Director's protected sales time.

Booking: all meetings run through the HubSpot scheduler (meetings.hubspot.com/pat-scally) so calendars, reminders and CRM records stay in sync with no admin.

First contact to signed engagement

The sales process, end to end

Seven steps carry a prospect from attention to a signed engagement — and beyond, into expansion. Marketing feeds the top; delivery takes the bottom.

1
Attract

Campaigns, LinkedIn and insight content (Marketing Playbook) put the problem in front of the right executives.

2
Capture

Landing pages and free assessments capture interest straight into HubSpot as a new lead with context.

3
Qualify

Score against the six tests. Pursue, park or decline — before booking MD time.

4
Discover

A senior conversation to confirm the problem, outcomes, sponsor, budget and timing; notes captured in HubSpot.

5
Propose

Claude Design drafts from the discovery notes; the MD tailors it; PandaDoc assembles, sends and tracks it.

6
Close

Handle questions, finalise scope and price within guardrails, and sign electronically in PandaDoc.

7
Onboard & expand

Hand to delivery, then look for the next need — cross-sell Governance Manager, assessments and repeat work.

The proposal engine

Proposals: HubSpot · Claude Design · PandaDoc

The three tools work as one chain — the CRM holds the context, Claude Design writes the first draft, and PandaDoc turns it into a tracked, signable document. This is how 0.5 FTE produces proposals quickly.

HubSpot

CRM, deals, meeting scheduler, sequences and discovery notes — the single source of truth.

Claude Design

Drafts the proposal narrative and structure from discovery notes and our templates.

PandaDoc

Assembles the branded proposal, pricing tables and e-signature; tracks opens and status.

The workflow — target: proposal out within 3 working days of discovery
#StepWhat happens
1CaptureDiscovery notes, scope and budget logged against the deal in HubSpot.
2DraftClaude Design produces a first-draft proposal from the notes and the relevant template.
3TailorMD reviews, sharpens the value story, and confirms scope, approach and price.
4AssembleContent moves into the PandaDoc template with pricing tables and terms.
5Send & trackPandaDoc sends for e-signature; opens and status sync back to the HubSpot deal.
6Sign & closeSigned document closes the deal won; delivery is notified and onboarding begins.

Maintain a small library of PandaDoc templates and Claude Design prompts per offer (advisory review, GM subscription, assessment package) so most proposals are assembled, not written from scratch.

Commercial discipline

Pricing & commercial guardrails

Consistent pricing protects margin and speeds proposals. These are the standing guardrails; anything outside them needs MD sign-off.

Rate card (excl GST)
CEO / Managing Director$259
Senior Advisor (AUS)$220
Advisor (PH)$200
Junior Advisor$123

Increase time-and-materials rates ~5% on CUAs. Rates reviewed annually.

Deal sizing
  • Target average advisory project: $35K (up from $25K).
  • At least one large project (>$50K) running at any time.
  • Assessments ($500–$5K) as low-risk entry points.
  • Favour recurring GM subscription where it fits.
Guardrails
  • Protect the 25% gross-profit floor on every engagement.
  • Fixed-price only with a clear scope; change requests are re-quoted.
  • Any discount beyond 10% needs MD approval.
  • Standard payment terms; deposits on larger fixed-price work.
Procurement routes
  • Common Use Arrangements (CUAs) — honour panel rates and rules.
  • WALGA panel — pursue membership for local-government access.
  • Direct engagement below procurement thresholds.
  • Check the buyer's route early — it shapes price and paperwork.
