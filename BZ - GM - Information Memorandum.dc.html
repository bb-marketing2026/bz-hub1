01 · Executive summary

A productised governance methodology, ready to scale

bluezoo is offering for sale Governance Manager — a wholly owned, cloud-based software-as-a-service platform that helps organisations understand, measure, manage and improve governance across a wide range of disciplines. The platform converts the often subjective practice of governance review into a structured, repeatable, evidence-based capability-maturity assessment, supported by an extensive library of frameworks, benchmarking, improvement planning, reporting and a substantial multimedia learning library.

Governance Manager is established, proven in market and revenue-generating. FY26 revenue was A$239,141, of which 63% was recurring, earned across three channels — a small number of large recurring-contract clients, direct one-off assessments, and a growing reseller network. The client base spans government and corporate organisations across Australia, several engaged via a partner network. The product is mature, with a decade of continuous development behind it, and is offered with the option to retain its long-serving development team to ensure continuity.

For bluezoo the platform sits outside its core advisory business, prompting a clean divestment. For the right acquirer — particularly a consulting or audit firm — it represents a rare opportunity to acquire a ready-built, defensible methodology and a recurring software revenue line that can be deployed immediately across an existing client base.

04 · Product overview

What Governance Manager does

Governance complexities can overwhelm even well-organised teams. Governance Manager addresses this with a single, integrated platform that helps organisations measure where they stand, decide what to improve, and prove progress over time — without the cost and inconsistency of bespoke consulting reviews.

At its heart, the platform solves a resource-effectiveness problem: it helps organisations identify needs and focus finite resources on the vital practices that form the next foundational layer of improvement. Maturity models give a structured way to evaluate an organisation’s current state, desired state, mandatory state and benchmark performance — turning judgement into measurable, comparable data.

Core capabilities

Framework library

An extensive, curated library of governance frameworks and capability standards reflecting industry best practice — deployable to suit each organisation’s priorities.

Maturity scoring

Flexible, maturity-based scoring models let teams assess capabilities consistently and chart the path from current to desired and mandatory states.

Benchmarking

Visually compare maturity across any number of entities — companies, projects, business units, suppliers — for deeper insight into relative performance.

Improvement programs

Assign, prioritise, schedule, monitor and report on improvement actions with full traceability of decision-making — from finding to outcome.

Reporting & analytics

Detailed reports and a comprehensive reference library let users dive into data and analytics to support informed, defensible decisions.

Learning library

Hundreds of hours of engaging video content break complex governance concepts into digestible segments — accelerating adoption across teams.

“When everyone in an organisation — or across an entire sector — can evaluate their business practices, incredible things happen.” Governance Manager is built to operate at scale, with enterprise-grade data and security controls.

07 · Market opportunity

A structural growth market

Governance, risk and compliance (GRC) demand is rising structurally. Boards and executives face intensifying regulatory scrutiny, heightened expectations around accountability and transparency, ESG obligations, cyber and data risk, and growing supply-chain complexity. Across both government and corporate sectors, organisations increasingly need a defensible, evidence-based way to measure and improve how they govern.

Governance Manager’s addressable market is effectively unlimited: because the platform is discipline-agnostic, any organisation in any sector that wants to assess and improve a capability is a potential user. Demand can be deepened within existing clients (more frameworks, more business units) and broadened across new sectors and geographies.

Regulatory pressure

Rising compliance and accountability obligations drive demand for structured assurance.

Do-more-with-less

Organisations must focus finite resources on the practices that matter most.

Benchmark demand

Sector bodies and groups increasingly want to compare maturity at scale.

For software providers

09 · Case studies in market

Proven across government & whole sectors

Governance Manager has been deployed not only for individual organisations but at whole-of-sector scale — coordinated by peak bodies and government departments to assess, benchmark and improve capability across dozens of entities at once. Two representative deployments are summarised below.

Not-for-profit governance · Western Australia
National Disability Services (NDS WA)

NDS is Australia’s peak body for non-government disability service organisations; in WA roughly 80 members operate several hundred services. With sector governance under close stakeholder scrutiny — where the failings of one provider can carry across the whole sector — NDS adopted Governance Manager as a web-based self-assessment tool offered as a value-added member service, with whole-of-sector performance reporting.

It lets providers and their boards assess current capability and set goals across governance areas — boards and committees, financial management, ethics and integrity, social capital, people management, risk and communications — and track “agreed actions” toward their goals.

ICT capability benchmarking · WA Government
Sport 4 All — Department of Sport & Recreation

Sport 4 All is a four-year, A$20 million program to lift participation in community sport across WA. Through its ClubTalk component, the Department of Sport and Recreation used Governance Manager in partnership with all 93 sporting associations to assess, compare and improve ICT capability across the sector — directly informing the discharge of the multi-million-dollar grant program.

Assessments used a purpose-built Small Business ICT Governance Framework (Plan & Organise, Acquire & Implement, Delivery & Support, Monitor & Evaluate), scored on a 1–4 capability-maturity scale — where the goal is the right level for each organisation, not simply the highest.

10 · People & transition

Continuity built into the deal

A common risk in acquiring niche software is loss of institutional knowledge. Governance Manager mitigates this directly: its senior developer — a very experienced engineer based in the Philippines who has worked on the platform for roughly the last decade — together with a support resource, are available to transfer with the asset (one senior developer FTE and one support FTE).

  • Deep, end-to-end knowledge of the codebase, frameworks and client configurations.
  • Optional retention of the senior developer at an ongoing cost of approximately A$60,000 per year if the buyer chooses; the support resource may also transfer.
  • Spare capacity beyond maintenance — able to take on new development and roadmap work for the acquirer.
  • Materially de-risks handover and protects service continuity for existing clients.

The Vendor will work with the acquirer on a sensible transition plan — including knowledge transfer, client introductions and a handover period — to be agreed as part of the transaction. Specifics are open to negotiation.
