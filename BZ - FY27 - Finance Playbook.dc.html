Significant accounting policies · I

Basis & income items

Basis of preparation

General purpose statements on the accrual basis and historical cost, modified where applicable by fair-value measurement of selected non-current assets, financial assets and liabilities.

Goods & Services Tax (GST)

Revenues, expenses and assets are recognised net of GST, except where GST is not recoverable from the ATO. Receivables and payables are stated inclusive of GST. Cash flows are presented gross; GST on investing/financing flows is shown as operating.

Cash & cash equivalents

Cash on hand, cash at bank, deposits on demand and highly liquid investments maturing within three months. Bank overdrafts are shown as short-term borrowings in current liabilities.

Trade & other receivables

Amounts due from customers for unpaid invoices and services. Those due within 12 months are current. Collectability is reviewed on an ongoing basis; uncollectible debts are written off when identified, and an allowance for doubtful debts is raised on objective evidence.

Trade & other payables

Liabilities for goods and services received before year end that are unpaid. Unsecured, recognised as a current liability, and normally paid within 30 days of recognition.

Grants & other contributions

Recognised as revenue when the business obtains control of the contributed assets, with attention to any conditions requiring the funds to be spent in a particular manner or period.

Significant accounting policies · II

Assets

Fixed assets

Each class is carried at cost or fair value less accumulated depreciation or impairment. Assets are initially recognised at cost — the fair value of consideration given plus incidental acquisition costs. Subsequent costs are capitalised only when future economic benefits are probable and cost is reliably measurable; other repairs and maintenance are expensed as incurred.

Capitalisation threshold

Expenditure on items of equipment under $1,000 is generally not capitalised; instead it is recorded on an asset inventory listing.

Revaluation

Revalued assets are carried at fair value. Increases are credited to a revaluation surplus in equity; decreases first offset prior increases for the same asset, then go to profit or loss. Revaluations occur with sufficient regularity that carrying amounts do not differ materially from fair value.

Depreciation of non-current assets

All non-current assets with a limited useful life (excluding freehold land) are depreciated on a straight-line basis over their useful lives, from the date of acquisition or completion. Residual values and useful lives are reviewed each reporting period. Gains and losses on disposal are recognised in comprehensive income.

Impairment

Assets (other than inventories) are assessed at each reporting date for indicators of impairment. Where indicated, the recoverable amount — the higher of fair value less costs to sell and value in use — is compared to carrying amount, and any excess is recognised immediately in profit or loss (or as a revaluation decrease for revalued assets).

Significant accounting policies · III

Liabilities & presentation

Employee benefits

Provision is made for benefits arising from services rendered to the end of the reporting period. Benefits expected to settle within a year are measured at the amounts expected to be paid; those beyond a year at the present value of estimated future cash outflows, allowing for wage increases and vesting probability.

Provisions

Recognised when there is a present legal or constructive obligation from a past event, an outflow of economic benefits is probable, and the amount can be reliably measured — using the best estimate to settle the obligation at reporting date.

Leases

Finance leases (substantially all risks and benefits transferred) are capitalised as an asset and liability and depreciated over the shorter of useful life or lease term. Operating lease payments are expensed as incurred; lease incentives are amortised over the lease term.

Current & non-current classification

Assets and liabilities are classified as current when expected to be settled within the 12-month operational cycle. Liabilities without an unconditional right to defer settlement beyond 12 months (e.g. vested long service leave) are current even if not expected to settle within the year.

Rounding off figures

All figures in the annual financial report, other than a rate in the dollar, are rounded to the nearest dollar.

Budget comparative figures

Unless otherwise stated, budget comparatives relate to the original budget estimate for the relevant item of disclosure.

Keeping it clean & solvent

Controls, month-end & reporting

Day-to-day discipline and a reliable month-end close are what let the CEO report accurate numbers to the Board and attest to solvency each month.

Standing controls
  • Chart-of-accounts changes: CEO approval only.
  • System access requires completed training.
  • Payables normally paid within 30 days.
  • Equipment under $1,000 to the asset inventory, not capitalised.
  • Receivables reviewed for collectability on an ongoing basis.
Month-end close
  • Reconcile bank, debtors, creditors and clearing accounts in Xero.
  • Post depreciation, accruals and payroll liabilities.
  • Confirm GST, PAYG and superannuation positions.
  • Refresh reporting (Reach) and cash forecast (Frog).
  • Produce the numbers for the Monthly Board Report.
The link to governance

A clean close feeds the Monthly Board Report — the strategic scorecard, budget compliance and the CEO's attestation of solvency. Good finance discipline is what makes "no surprises to the Board" possible.

  1. Go to the ASIC officeholder portal (asic.gov.au/company-online) and log in.
  2. Key in the ACN / ABN — Blue Zoo Holdings Pty Ltd, ACN 119 062 850.

Appendix B

Accounts payable

Two types: a traditional supplier invoice, and a debit-card transaction.

Supplier invoice
  1. Receive the invoice on delivery of goods/services; confirm goods received and in good condition.
  2. Save the invoice to OneDrive as "YYYYMMDD Supplier Name-Purchase Summary-Amount".
  3. Log into Xero and create a new bill from the New menu; then proceed to bank processing and reconciliation.
Debit-card transaction
  1. The card owner saves the receipt to OneDrive using the same naming format.
  2. Draft the bill in Xero; open the dashboard and click "Reconcile items" for the card.
  3. Input the bill details, attach the receipt from OneDrive, and click Approve. Proceed to reconciliation.

Appendix C

Reimbursements & accounts receivable

Reimbursements (employee expense claims)
  1. Claims are sent periodically, in a single email, at least three days before the pay run (Wednesdays, fortnightly).
  2. Staff incur the expense on personal cash/card and email receipts to the accountant.
  3. Check validity; every Monday of pay-run week, draft a bill in Xero payable to the staff member.
  4. Send bank payment on the pay-run date.
Accounts receivable
  1. The CEO sends the billing sheet on or before each invoicing run (15th and 28th of the month).
  2. In Xero, click the plus icon then Invoice; encode the information from the billing sheet.
  3. Click Save & Submit, forward to the CEO for approval, then send invoices to customers.
  4. Monitor payments after the due date; if unpaid, proceed to delinquent collections.
GL categories include Consulting Fees, Wizer Licences, Reimbursements (on-billed client costs) and Governance Manager.

Appendix D

Delinquent collections

  1. For invoices past due, discuss the missed payment with the CEO.
  2. If the cause is an invoice issue, investigate and resend the corrected invoice.
  3. If there is no issue, at 14 days overdue get CEO approval and send the first email reminder via Xero.
  4. If no payment or commitment a week later, call the customer.
  5. At 28 days overdue, send the second email reminder via Xero.
  6. Inform the CEO; the CEO personally contacts the customer. Last resort is a statutory demand.
Reminder template: a courteous note acknowledging difficult trading conditions, stating the overdue balance and days past due, inviting the customer to discuss a workable solution, and requesting payment or communication.

Appendix F

Superannuation & salary sacrifice

Superannuation
  1. In Xero → Payroll → Superannuation, click Add Super Payment.
  2. Choose the lines of the covered month from the posted pay runs (paid monthly to manage cash flow, though due quarterly).
  3. Click Submit for Approval; the batch shows as 'Pending Approval'.
  4. The Finance Director receives an SMS authorisation code (valid 24 hours); the approver opens the batch, clicks Approve, enters the code and clicks Approve & Submit.
Salary sacrifice
  1. Staff advise their line manager of intent to salary sacrifice; the LM consults the accountant on cash-flow impact.
  2. If allowable, LM and staff finalise terms and sign the agreement (staff may not resign until the asset is fully paid).
  3. The asset is purchased via debit card or bank processing; the accountant updates the payroll template and sets it up in Xero.

Bank processing & attestation

  1. After creating the bill in Xero, download its .aba file.

Appendix I

Bank reconciliation

  1. Ensure all payable bills and transactions are approved, and all receivable invoices collected.
  2. Check that approved bills and collected invoices match the bank lines on the statement.
  3. Open the Xero dashboard and click "Reconcile items" for the account being reconciled.
  4. Beside the bank line, in the Match tab click Find & Match; select the matching bill.
  5. For international transactions, split the transaction to match the bill across both bank lines (transaction fee appears separately).
  6. Click OK to finish matching; move to the next line until all are matched.
  7. If bills do not match, review invoices and amend bills as necessary.

Company tax & BAS

Company tax return

Blue Zoo uses Marsden Stantons to prepare and lodge the company return; initiated after 30 June reconciliation.

  1. Reconcile the books at the end of the financial year and advise Marsden Stantons (they have Xero access).
  2. The Finance Director signs the Letter of Engagement to initiate the review.
  3. Provide information as required (e.g. tax statements from InvestSmart & Colonial First State).
  4. Monitor progress; follow up if not filed by February. Marsden Stantons files the return.
Business Activity Statement (BAS)
  1. Ensure all cash and bank transactions are reconciled and pay runs posted.
  2. In Xero → Accounting → Reports → Activity Statement, click Review for the reporting period.
  3. Review BAS figures for PAYG against the Payroll Summary Report.
  4. Click Lodge to ATO, tick Authorise to file, and Lodge. A refund goes to the operating account; a payable is processed via bank processing.

Appendix M

Budget procedures

The budget is updated twice a year against a four-year planning horizon (the Strategic Plan), with forecasting and reporting to maintain budgetary control.

End-year (delivered June, operational 1 July)
  1. Commence 1 April: review prior budget and variances; understand strategic decisions with budget impact.
  2. Assess likely future revenues, fixed costs and variable costs; prepare a draft budget by 30 April.
  3. Scrutinise cash flows for sustainability; deliver the draft to the CEO with scenarios.
  4. Implement Board changes by mid-May; deliver the final budget for the June Board agenda.
  5. Receive endorsement mid-June; publish in the finance system by end June; advise the CEO the budget is live by 1 July.
Mid-year (delivered December, operational 1 January)
  1. Commence 1 October: review the period and variances and any strategic budget impacts.
  2. Reassess revenues and costs; prepare draft amendments and test against cash flow.
  3. Deliver draft amendments to the CEO with scenarios by mid-October; implement Board changes and republish.
Budget policy: operate financially sustainably — surpluses fund capital, Future Fund allocations and debt servicing, and provide for calculated risk-taking and innovation.
