AI governance for WA government agencies

Moving from scattered experimentation to Board-level oversight — a practical guide for directors and executives, before AI becomes an audit finding.

By Pat Scally · Chief Executive Officer, Blue Zoo

Across the Western Australian public sector, AI has already arrived — not through a Cabinet decision or an ICT roadmap, but through staff quietly adopting tools that draft, summarise and analyse. The technology moved from novelty to daily practice without pausing at the board table. The result is a widening gap between what agencies are using and what they are actually governing, and it is precisely where the next adverse finding is most likely to sit.

The gap that is quietly widening
AI adoption — staff using it in daily work~70%
AI governance — agencies with formal arrangements~1 in 5

The distance between the two bars is the governance gap. It is not a technology problem — the tools work. It is an oversight problem: adoption has outpaced accountability, and the Auditor General has already signalled that AI governance is firmly in scope. Most WA agencies today sit at level 1 or 2 — experimenting without a policy, an inventory or a named owner. The destination is not level 5 everywhere; it is a consistent, defensible level 3 across the domains that matter most.

An operating model that works

Four accountable layers — no new bureaucracy

Good AI governance does not need a new committee for everything. It needs clear lines: who sets the appetite, who approves the risky uses, who owns each system, and who does the day-to-day. Assurance sits alongside, independent.

Board / DG

Sets AI risk appetite; holds ultimate accountability

→
AI Governance Committee

Approves high-consequence uses; owns the AI register

→
System owners

Accountable for each system's outcomes & controls

→
All staff

Use within policy; register tools; escalate risks

Assurance (Audit & Risk Committee): independent line of sight over the whole model — tests that the controls above are real, not just documented, and aligns to ISO/IEC 42001.
The director's AI readiness checklist
Know & control
✓We hold a current inventory of where AI is used, and by whom
✓Every significant AI system has one named, accountable owner
✓Sensitive data cannot be entered into unapproved tools
✓We know where our data goes and whether it trains external models
Decide & assure
✓High-consequence uses need approval and an impact assessment
✓AI-assisted decisions can be explained and are recorded
✓A human remains accountable for decisions affecting people
✓AI governance is reviewed at least annually as rules evolve
Four questions to ask this quarter
1 · Where is AI already being used here?
2 · Who is accountable when it gets something wrong?
3 · Could we explain an AI-assisted decision?
4 · Where does the information we enter actually go?
