The campaign

You represent the sector. Could you describe how well it is governed?

A peak body speaks for its members to government, funders and the public. It is expected to know the state of the sector it represents — yet on governance, most peak bodies are working from anecdote: the members who ask for help, and the ones who appear in the media.

This campaign positions Governance Manager as a governance aggregator. Members self‑assess against the frameworks that matter to the sector; the peak body sees the aggregate. That produces three things no peak body currently has: a sector benchmark, an evidenced view of common weaknesses where development effort would yield most, and identified centres of best practice worth celebrating and learning from.

Value proposition

“Lead the sector with evidence — a governance benchmark for your members, and a map of where sector development effort will pay.”

The offer

A free pilot: one framework domain, assessed across up to 10 volunteer members, with an anonymised sector snapshot.

The call to action

A 45‑minute conversation to scope the pilot cohort, then the snapshot presented to your board.

Why this lands with peak bodies

Contagion is a real fear. A significant governance failure at one member damages the whole sector's standing. Peak bodies know this and have almost no early‑warning mechanism.

It is a genuine member benefit. Peak bodies are constantly seeking value‑added services that justify membership. Each member gets their own confidential maturity profile and improvement plan.

It strengthens their advocacy. Arguing for sector funding or against a regulatory change is far stronger with evidence about member capability than with assertion.

Precedent exists. NDS, the Public Sector Commission, the Lusaka Stock Exchange and the Department of Sport and Recreation have all done exactly this. Lead with those.

Positioning discipline: never frame this as the peak body auditing its members. Peak bodies exist to serve members, not police them — and a compliance framing will be refused. Frame it as a member benefit that produces aggregate insight as a by‑product, with individual results confidential to the member.

Section one

Ideal customer profile

The best fit is a body with a defined membership it can convene, a mandate to lift standards, and either a funding relationship with government or a public‑interest obligation that makes sector reputation material.

Attribute
Ideal profile
Body type
Peak bodies and industry associations, sector regulators, licensing and accreditation bodies, funding departments, stock exchanges, member federations, local government associations, professional institutes
Membership size
20–300 member organisations — enough for a benchmark to be meaningful, few enough that the peak body can convene them personally
Sectors
Disability and community services, health and aged care, sport and recreation, local government, education and training, housing, environment, primary industry, financial services
Mandate signals
A code of practice, accreditation standard or sector governance guideline it already publishes; a member capability‑building program; a stated advocacy role
Funding context
Government funding or grant administration — either receiving it for sector development, or distributing it to members (as with Sport 4 All)
Trigger events
A member governance failure in the media; a funding submission for sector capability building; a code of practice being written or revised; a new CEO or policy director; an accreditation scheme launching; a government review of the sector
Disqualifiers
Fewer than ~15 members; a purely commercial trade group with no standards mandate; a body in open conflict with its membership
Best entry point
The sector development or member services function, not the advocacy function — they own capability building and are measured on member value

Buying roles

Primary — the champion

Chief Executive, Executive Director, or Director Sector Development / Member Services. Owns member value and can authorise a pilot without a board resolution.

Economic buyer

The peak body board, or the funding department behind it. Sector programs are frequently funded by government rather than from member fees — identify the funder early.

Influencer

Policy or Advocacy Director, and two or three respected member CEOs. A pilot endorsed by well‑regarded members converts the rest of the membership faster than the peak body can alone.

Blocker to expect

Member resistance to being “assessed by head office”. Answer with confidentiality architecture: the member owns their result, the peak body sees only the aggregate.

Section two

The peak body leader, in their own terms

A peak body chief executive is balancing member loyalty, government expectations and sector reputation with a small team and uncertain funding. They respond to anything that demonstrably serves members while strengthening the body's own standing.

What keeps them awake
A member failure that damages the whole sector's standing
Being asked by a minister how the sector is performing
Advocating for funding with anecdote rather than evidence
Member value questioned at renewal time
A code of practice nobody can show is being followed
Development effort spread thin instead of aimed at real gaps
What they want instead
A sector governance benchmark, refreshed annually
Early sight of weaknesses common across members
Named centres of best practice to showcase and share
A member service that visibly justifies the fee
Evidence behind every funding and policy submission
Demonstrated leadership of the sector, not just representation

Objections and honest answers

Objection
Response
“Our members won't agree to be assessed.”
They're not being assessed by you. Each member self‑assesses, owns their own result and gets their own improvement plan. You see the aggregate only — anonymised, never member‑identified.
“We're a member body, not a regulator.”
Exactly why this works. It's a capability service, not a compliance exercise. NDS offers it as a value‑added member benefit; the sector view is a by‑product.
“We can't fund this from member fees.”
Most don't. Sport 4 All was funded by the Department; NDS by the Disability Services Commission. We'll help build the funding submission — sector capability building is fundable work.
“Our members are too diverse to compare.”
Diversity is handled by cohorting — compare by size, service type or region. And maturity is about the right level for each organisation, not a single score everyone is held to.
“What if the results are poor?”
Then you've found the sector development priority before a regulator or journalist did — which is precisely the position a peak body wants to be in.

Section three

How the aggregator works

Members assess themselves; the peak body sees the sector. Multiple frameworks can run across the same membership, so a single program covers corporate governance, risk, policy, ICT and remuneration without a separate exercise for each.

Step 01
Select frameworks

Choose the domains that matter to the sector — or deploy the peak body's own code of practice as the reference model.

Step 02
Members self‑assess

Each member scores capability and evidence online, and receives their own confidential maturity profile and action plan.

Step 03
Aggregate & benchmark

Results roll up anonymously into a sector benchmark, with cohorting by size, service type or region.

Step 04
Target & celebrate

Aim sector development at common weaknesses; identify and showcase pockets of excellence; re‑measure annually.

The federated assessment functionality is the mechanism — the same capability the energy client uses to compare power stations, applied across a membership instead of a group.

What the peak body gets

A sector benchmark

An initial baseline that sets the standard, published back to members — then a trend line as it is re‑run each year.

Common weaknesses, ranked

The domains where most members score low — the places sector‑wide training, templates or guidance will yield the greatest return.

Centres of best practice

Members scoring highly in a domain, identified with their consent — to celebrate, and to share their approach with the rest.

Evidence for advocacy

Defensible sector data for funding submissions, policy responses and ministerial briefings — and for demonstrating sector improvement over time.

Section four

Campaign mechanics

A longer cycle than the individual‑role campaigns. Peak body decisions involve a board and often a funder, so plan on a six‑week sequence per cohort and a three to nine month close — against materially larger contract values.

Weeks 1–2
Reach

Intro email from Pat with the closest case study attached; LinkedIn posts 1–4; warm introductions through sector networks.

Weeks 3–4
Scope

Follow‑up email; posts 5–8; 45‑minute scoping calls to agree frameworks, cohort and funding route.

Weeks 5–6
Pilot

Pilot runs with up to 10 volunteer members; posts 9–12; anonymised snapshot produced.

Weeks 7+
Convert

Snapshot presented to the board; proposal for the full membership program, with funding submission support.

Run one cohort of peak bodies per quarter alongside the role‑based campaigns — not monthly. Time approaches to the sector's funding submission season, when capability‑building money is being sought.

Lower volume, materially higher value. One sector program can exceed a year of individual assessment sales, and it converts an entire membership into a warm audience for the role‑based campaigns.
