The campaign

Is your project governance strong enough to survive the project?

Project Directors are measured on delivery, but the thing most likely to derail a major project is not execution — it is governance: unclear decision rights, drifting strategic alignment, contested resource calls, and a steering committee that receives activity reporting rather than decisions.

This campaign offers project leadership an objective, evidence-based read on the governance wrapped around their portfolio — using the PROJECTassess Framework in Governance Manager. It is deliberately not a project-management-tool pitch. Project Directors already have delivery tooling; what they lack is a defensible measure of governance quality they can take to a steering committee or an assurance gate.

Value proposition

“Improve execution and time‑to‑value by strengthening the governance around delivery.”

The offer

A free assessment against one domain of the PROJECTassess Framework, on one live project — their choice of the five.

The call to action

Assess one domain in under an hour, then a 30‑minute debrief with a Blue Zoo adviser.

Why this lands with Project Directors

It speaks to their exposure, not ours. Project Directors carry personal accountability when a project fails, and they know that post‑mortems almost always name governance. An early, private read on that exposure is genuinely useful to them.

It arrives before the gate, not after. Assurance reviews, gateway reviews and audits all assess project governance. PROJECTassess lets a director see what those reviewers will see, while there is still time to act.

It is fast and low‑commitment. One project, one domain, under an hour. No procurement, no platform rollout, no exposure to their executive until they choose.

It produces something they can use. The output is a maturity profile and ranked gap list — material that strengthens a steering‑committee paper rather than adding to their workload.

Positioning discipline: we are not selling project management. We are offering a governance instrument that makes an existing delivery capability more defensible — and that reads as respect for their expertise rather than a critique of it.

Decision rights that are unclear until a decision is contested
A steering committee that reviews status but does not decide

A gateway or audit review arriving before the gaps are closed
Resource commitments made verbally across competing projects

A defensible baseline they can show an assurance reviewer
Clarity on who decides what, written down and agreed
Early warning rather than late escalation
A steering committee that makes decisions, on the record
Evidence that alignment to strategy is real, not asserted
Less time preparing for reviews, more time delivering

Section three

What PROJECTassess actually measures

Five governance capabilities, each scored for maturity and for the confidence with which it could be evidenced — so a Project Director can see both how strong a practice is and how well it would stand up to review.

Framework
Roles, responsibilities and processes for project management and oversight are defined, and every project is initiated, planned, executed and closed within that structure.
Strategic alignment
Projects are evaluated and selected on strategic value, and the portfolio is prioritised toward the initiatives that deliver the greatest organisational benefit.
Resource management
Resources are planned, tracked and optimised across projects to meet deadlines and budgets — maximising utilisation and reducing conflict and waste.
Stakeholder communication
Stakeholders receive regular updates and are actively involved in project decisions, with feedback and collaboration built into the governance rhythm.
Compliance monitoring
Relevant regulations and standards are monitored through the project lifecycle, with compliance checks and audit‑ready reporting rather than end‑of‑project scramble.

The outcomes we can credibly promise

Improved project outcomes

Better alignment, resourcing and stakeholder engagement lift the odds of on‑time, on‑budget delivery to the required quality.

Enhanced decision‑making

Decision‑makers get a clear view of progress, resource use and compliance status, so issues are addressed early rather than escalated late.

Earlier risk mitigation

A systematic governance read identifies risks at each lifecycle stage, so projects meet fewer unexpected issues.

Stronger compliance posture

Regulatory requirements are tracked through delivery, reducing exposure at gateway reviews and audits.

Campaign mechanics

A four‑week sequence per cohort, run by the BDM with Pat Scally on the debriefs. Everything is tracked in HubSpot against the standard deal stages.

Week 1
Reach

Intro email to the cohort; LinkedIn posts 1–3; connection requests to Project Directors.

Week 2
Engage

Follow‑up email; posts 4–6; connected calls to book domain assessments. Minimum 20 connected calls.

Week 3
Assess

Domain assessments run; posts 7–9; profiles produced and debriefs scheduled.

Week 4
Convert

30‑minute debriefs; posts 10–12; proposals for portfolio‑wide assessment packs.

One cohort per month. The sequence repeats with a fresh list; posts continue between cohorts to keep the topic warm.
