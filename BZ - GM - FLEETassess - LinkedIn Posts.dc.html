Post 02 · Age vs whole-of-life

A question worth asking your own team this week. Why are we replacing that vehicle? If the honest answer is "it's reached the age in the policy", you have a replacement schedule — not lifecycle governance. Whole-of-life cost and condition data change replacement decisions materially, and they change the funding conversation even more. Age is the easiest justification available and the weakest one under scrutiny. Lifecycle management is one of four capabilities FLEETassess measures. Post 03 · Compliance timing

There are two ways to handle fleet compliance. One: obligations are tracked continuously, so the evidence exists before anyone asks for it. Two: it's assembled in the fortnight before an audit, from three systems and a spreadsheet somebody maintains privately. Most fleets run the second and describe it as the first. The gap between them isn't administrative — it's the difference between a defensible position and a scramble. Free FLEETassess governance baseline. Under an hour, no data extracts.

Post 05 · Utilisation

Most fleets can tell you how many vehicles they have. Far fewer can tell you, with evidence, which ones are genuinely needed. Utilisation is the most quoted and least governed number in fleet. It gets asserted in budget papers and rarely tested — which means the fleet carries assets nobody has justified in years, and the Fleet Manager wears the cost criticism for all of them. Post 06 · Above the system

A fair question I get from Fleet Managers: "We already have a fleet management system. Why assess governance separately?" Because they answer different questions. Your system tells you what the fleet did — kilometres, fuel, services, faults. Governance determines whether the right assets were bought, who decides what, whether obligations are tracked, and whether anyone would know early if something was going wrong. FLEETassess reads the governance around your systems. It doesn't replace any of them. Free assessment on one domain on your fleet.

Post 07 · EV transition

Most fleet EV transition plans are procurement plans wearing a strategy label. Vehicle selection, charging infrastructure, depot works — all necessary, all operational. What's usually missing is the governance: who decides the transition sequence, on what evidence, against which approved strategic objective, and how the whole-of-life case is tested as prices and technology move. A transition without governance is a series of purchases you'll be asked to justify later. Free FLEETassess assessment on one domain before you commit the capital.

Post 08 · Chain of responsibility

Chain of Responsibility doesn't stop at the driver. It reaches the people who scheduled the work, approved the maintenance regime, set the utilisation expectations and signed the procurement. In a review, all of those are governance decisions — and all of them need a documented basis. The uncomfortable question isn't whether your fleet is compliant. It's whether you could demonstrate it, quickly, for decisions made two years ago. Free FLEETassess governance baseline. The result is yours alone.

Post 09 · Capability vs evidence

Here's a test that separates confident fleet governance from defensible fleet governance. Take any practice you're sure about — maintenance scheduling, driver checks, disposal approvals. Now ask: if an auditor asked us to prove this today, what would we hand over? A strong practice with weak evidence isn't good governance yet. It's good intentions waiting to be documented. FLEETassess scores every element twice: how well it's embedded, and how readily you could prove it. The gap between them is usually the most useful finding. Free assessment on one domain available.
