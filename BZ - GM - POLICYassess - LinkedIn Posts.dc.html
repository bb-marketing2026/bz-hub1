Post 02 · Clarity test

A test worth running on your own policy library. Hand any policy to a new staff member and ask them what they must do, and what's out of scope. If they can't answer from the document alone, the policy isn't clear — regardless of how well it's written or how current it is. Clarity is measured by what the reader can act on, not by what the author intended. Policy development and clarity is one of four POLICYassess domains. Post 03 · The unknown policy

Here's where most policy frameworks quietly fail. A policy that is current, compliant and beautifully written but unknown to the people it governs will still be found not to have been followed. And that's a governance finding, not a communications one. Engagement isn't the soft end of policy work — it's the domain that determines whether any of the rest of it mattered. Engagement and implementation is one of four POLICYassess domains. Free assessment on just that one.

Post 04 · Legislative change

When legislation changes, how does it reach your policy framework? In a lot of organisations the honest answer is: someone happens to read about it, and remembers which policy it touches. That works until the person who remembers moves on. Mapping each policy to the obligation it exists to satisfy is what turns legislative monitoring from an individual's diligence into a system. Post 05 · The review date

For most policies, "review" is triggered by the date on the cover page. Which means the question being asked is "is this document overdue?" rather than "is this policy working?" Those are very different questions. The first is administrative. The second requires you to know whether the policy changed behaviour, produced consistent decisions, or created problems nobody has reported upward. Monitoring and improvement is one of four POLICYassess domains. Free assessment on one domain.

Post 06 · Orphaned policies

Every restructure creates orphaned policies. The role that owned the document no longer exists, or was split across two teams, and nobody formally inherited it. The policy stays in the library, cited in inductions, and quietly ownerless. Nobody notices until an auditor asks who approved it last, or an incident review asks who was responsible for keeping it current. Post 07 · Not a document system

To be clear about something, because the assumption is reasonable. This isn't a document management pitch. Storage is solved in most organisations — you have an intranet, a records system, version control. What's usually not solved is governance: who reviews what, when, on whose authority, whether it aligns to the obligation it serves, and how anyone knows staff are actually following it. Four domains, measured for maturity and for evidence. Free assessment on one of them.

Post 09 · Capability vs evidence

A test that separates confident policy governance from defensible policy governance. Take any part of the framework you're comfortable about. Now ask: if internal audit asked us to prove this today, what would we hand over? A strong practice with weak evidence isn't good governance yet. It's good intentions waiting to be documented. POLICYassess scores each domain twice — how well it's embedded, and how readily you could prove it. The gap between them is usually the most useful finding. Free assessment on one domain.

Post 10 · Practice vs policy

The most useful question in policy governance is also the least asked. Does practice match the policy? Not "is the policy current" or "is it approved" — does what people actually do resemble what the document says? Where it doesn't, one of two things is true: the policy is wrong, or the practice is. Both matter, and neither is visible from the register. Free POLICYassess assessment on one domain of your choosing, with a 30-minute debrief.
