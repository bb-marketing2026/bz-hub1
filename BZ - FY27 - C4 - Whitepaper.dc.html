Why compliance alone does not deliver better public sector performance

Moving from satisfying obligations to building the capability that improves outcomes.

Pat Scally
Chief Executive Officer, Blue Zoo
Governance · Strategy · Risk · Resilience
Introduction

Compliant, but not necessarily better

Public sector agencies have never been more compliant. Frameworks, policies and reporting obligations have multiplied for two decades. Yet more compliance has not reliably produced better services — and that gap is the quiet frustration of many executives.

The reason is that compliance and performance answer different questions. Compliance asks whether an agency followed the rules. Performance asks whether the public is better off. An agency can excel at the first and still struggle with the second, and many capable ones do.

This is not an argument against compliance. Rules exist for good reasons, and meeting them matters. It is an argument that compliance, on its own, is not enough — that satisfying obligations and improving outcomes are related but distinct, and that treating them as the same thing quietly caps how good an agency can become.

This paper sets out ten shifts that separate agencies which merely comply from those which genuinely improve. Together they describe the move from compliance to capability — from satisfying rules to delivering outcomes.

Compliance keeps an agency safe. Capability makes it good. The two are related — but they are not the same, and only one of them improves outcomes.

The ten shifts

The limits of compliance

01Compliance proves the process ran, not that it worked

Compliance answers a narrow question: did we follow the rule? It is a necessary discipline, but it is silent on the question that matters most — did following the rule actually improve anything? An agency can complete every mandatory step of a program and still deliver a poor outcome. Compliance confirms the machinery turned; it says nothing about whether it produced value.

02A clean audit is a floor, not a ceiling

A clean compliance result tells you the agency met the minimum expected of it. That is worth having, but it is the starting line, not the finish. Treating a clean audit as proof of good performance is one of the most common misreadings in the sector. The absence of findings is not the presence of excellence.

03Compliance measures activity; performance measures outcomes

Compliance frameworks count what is easy to count — policies in place, training completed, deadlines met. Performance asks what changed as a result. The two are frequently confused, and agencies report activity as though it were achievement. A wall of green indicators can sit comfortably alongside services that are not actually improving.

04Rules can be met while their intent is missed

Every rule exists for a reason, but the rule and its purpose are not the same thing. Under pressure, organisations optimise for the letter of a requirement and lose sight of its spirit — the procurement that is technically compliant but poor value, the report filed on time but never read. Capability holds on to why a rule exists.

Ask yourself: can we point to a single service that is measurably better this year because of something our governance told us — not just something we complied with?

What capability adds

05Capability turns controls into better decisions

A control is only valuable if it changes what people do. In a compliance mindset, controls are hurdles to clear; in a capability mindset, they are sources of insight that sharpen decisions. The same risk register that gathers dust as a compliance artefact can, used well, tell leaders exactly where to focus. The difference is not the control itself but the capability to use it.

06Risk becomes foresight, not paperwork

Treated as compliance, risk management produces registers that are updated to satisfy a cycle and forgotten between reviews. Treated as capability, it becomes the organisation's early-warning system — shaping what gets funded, deferred or escalated before problems arrive. The framework can look identical on paper; what differs is whether risk reaches the decisions it is meant to inform.

07Assurance becomes improvement, not fault-finding

Where internal audit is experienced as fault-finding, information is filtered and findings are resisted. Where it is treated as a source of insight, the same function drives genuine improvement. Capability reframes assurance from a test to be survived into a conversation about how to do better. Agencies that make that shift close their findings faster and learn more from each review.

"The absence of audit findings is not the presence of good performance. Compliance is the floor beneath capability, never a substitute for it."

Building the capability

08Measure governance maturity, not just compliance status

Compliance status is binary — met or not met. Maturity is a spectrum, and it is far more useful. Asking how well an agency governs, rather than merely whether it complied, reveals where capability is strong, where it is fragile, and where to invest. A maturity view gives boards something a checklist never can: the trajectory, not just the moment.

09Connect governance to strategy, delivery and outcomes

Governance delivers performance only when it is joined up with everything else — strategic planning, risk, delivery, and the outcomes the agency exists to achieve. Treated as a separate compliance stream, it becomes overhead. Connected to the business of the agency, it keeps strategy on track and turns intent into results. The connection is where compliance becomes capability.

10Make it continuous — capability is a habit, not a project

Compliance is episodic; it peaks around audits and reporting deadlines. Capability is continuous, built through the ordinary rhythm of how an agency makes decisions, reviews itself and acts on what it learns. Agencies that treat governance improvement as a one-off project regress as soon as it ends. Those that build it into their operating rhythm keep getting better.

What to do about it

From satisfying rules to delivering outcomes

None of this diminishes the importance of doing the basics well. Compliance protects the agency and the public, and no amount of capability compensates for a broken control. But compliance is where performance starts, not where it ends.

The agencies that stand out are not those with the most policies or the cleanest checklists. They are the ones that treat governance as a capability to be built — using their controls, their risk insight and their assurance to make better decisions and deliver better outcomes, year after year.

Moving from compliance to capability is not a large or expensive undertaking. It is a shift in mindset, reinforced by the right measures and the discipline to keep at it — the difference between an agency that satisfies its obligations and one that earns the confidence of the public it serves.
