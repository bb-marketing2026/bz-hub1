Governance done differently
Subject: The CEO appraisal — a more structured way through it
From: Pat Scally <pats@bluezoo.com.au>
To: [First name] [Last name], Chair, [Organisation]

Dear [First name],

I've chaired boards and advised many others, and I'd offer one observation that most Chairs recognise privately: the chief executive appraisal is the most consequential thing a board does each year, and often the least structured thing it does.

Not through any failing. It's simply that boards rarely have an instrument. So the discussion gets shaped by the most recent event, by the strength of individual relationships, and by whoever speaks first — and the outcome is a judgement that is genuinely held but difficult to evidence if it were ever challenged.

The CEOassess Framework in Governance Manager was built for that gap. It rates chief‑executive performance across six connected dimensions — corporate strategy, organisational alignment, team and processes, board engagement, external stakeholders, and the CEO's own personal practice — and records the evidence behind each rating. You needn't take on all six to start — choose the single dimension you most want a clearer read on. The judgement stays entirely with the board; the framework simply gives it structure and a trend line.

I'd like to offer you a free assessment against one dimension of the framework, in confidence, on whichever dimension you choose. Practically:

  • Under an hour of your time, completed by you as Chair
  • A maturity profile on that dimension, with the gaps ranked
  • A 30‑minute confidential debrief with me
  • Confidential to you — nothing goes to the CEO, the board or the organisation unless you send it

One point worth making early: most chief executives welcome this once they see it. Very few have ever received a structured read on their own performance, and CEOassess is as much a development instrument for them as an appraisal instrument for you. I'm happy to give the CEO their own access if that helps you introduce it.

Would a short call be worthwhile? If the appraisal isn't due for some months, that is arguably the better time to look at it — before expectations are set rather than after.

Send notes
  • Send from Pat, always. Chairs expect to deal with a principal.
  • “Dear” not “Hi” for this audience, and no contractions in the opening paragraph.
  • The CEO paragraph is essential — it removes the objection before the Chair raises it. Do not cut it to shorten the email.
  • Alternative subjects: “A structured basis for the CEO appraisal” · “Appraising the chief executive on evidence”.
  • Never imply the CEO is underperforming or the board is deficient. Follow up once after 5–7 business days.
