1Purpose

This policy establishes how [Agency name] governs the development, procurement and use of artificial intelligence (AI). It exists to ensure AI is used lawfully, ethically, transparently and safely; that accountability for AI-assisted decisions is clear; and that the agency can demonstrate responsible AI use to the Auditor General, oversight bodies and the public it serves.

2Scope

This policy applies to all officers, employees, contractors, consultants and third parties acting on behalf of the agency. It covers all AI systems whether built in-house, procured, or embedded within other products — including generative AI, machine learning, automated decision-making and analytics tools that materially influence a decision or output.

2.1Where this policy conflicts with legislation, a Public Sector Commissioner's Instruction or a whole-of-government directive, the higher instrument prevails.
2.2Personal use of publicly available AI tools on agency information is governed by clauses 6 and 7.
3Definitions
Artificial intelligence — systems that perform tasks normally requiring human intelligence, including generative and predictive models.
Automated decision-making — a decision made, or substantially made, by a system without meaningful human involvement.
High-consequence use — an AI use that affects a person's rights, entitlements, safety or access to services.
Human oversight — a competent officer able to review, override and be accountable for an AI-assisted output.
AI system owner — the named officer accountable for a specific AI system and its outcomes.
Shadow AI — AI tools used without approval, record or oversight.

4Guiding principles

All AI use by the agency must uphold the following principles. They apply whether an AI system is built, bought or embedded.

Accountable

A named officer is answerable for every significant AI system and its decisions.

Lawful & ethical

AI use complies with the law and the public sector code of conduct.

Transparent & explainable

AI-assisted decisions can be explained and are disclosed where they affect people.

Fair & unbiased

AI is tested for bias and must not entrench disadvantage.

Human-centred

Meaningful human oversight is retained for high-consequence decisions.

Secure & private

Data used with AI is protected, and sovereignty and privacy are preserved.

5Governance and responsibilities

6Acceptable use and risk classification

Before an AI system is used, its risk tier must be assessed and the appropriate approval obtained. The higher the consequence, the greater the oversight required.

Tier
Examples
Approval required
Low
Drafting, summarising non-sensitive text; brainstorming
Line manager; approved tools only
Medium
Analysing internal data; drafting advice that informs decisions
System owner; recorded in AI register
High
Influencing decisions about individuals, safety or compliance
AI Governance Committee; impact assessment
Prohibited
Fully automated decisions on rights/entitlements; sensitive data in public tools
Not permitted
6.1Only AI tools on the agency's approved-tools list may be used. Shadow AI is prohibited.
6.2High-consequence uses require a documented AI impact assessment before deployment.
6.3AI must not be used to fully automate a decision that materially affects a person's rights or entitlements without a human decision-maker.
7Data, privacy and security
7.1Personal, confidential, sensitive or classified information must not be entered into AI tools that are not approved for that data classification.
7.2Data sovereignty must be preserved; agency information must not be used to train external models without explicit approval.
7.3AI use must comply with privacy obligations, records management and the agency's information security framework.
7.4Data used by AI systems must be accurate, current, lawfully held and fit for purpose.

8Transparency and human oversight
8.1Where an AI system materially contributes to a decision affecting a person, that use must be disclosed in plain language.
8.2A competent officer must be able to explain, review and override any AI-assisted output before it is acted upon.
8.3The reasoning and evidence behind high-consequence AI-assisted decisions must be recorded and retained.
8.4AI-generated content used externally must be checked for accuracy and appropriateness by the responsible officer.
9Procurement and third parties
9.1AI systems and AI-enabled products must be assessed against this policy before procurement or deployment.
9.2Contracts must address data use, transparency, security, sovereignty, model training and exit arrangements.
9.3The agency remains accountable for outcomes produced by third-party AI; obligations do not transfer to the vendor.
10Capability and training
10.1Staff must complete AI awareness training appropriate to their role before using AI tools.
10.2Executives and board members receive AI governance briefings sufficient to exercise informed oversight.
10.3System owners must understand the capabilities, limitations and risks of the systems they own.
Alignment

This policy is structured to align with ISO/IEC 42001 (AI management systems) and supports the agency's obligations under privacy, records, information security and public sector integrity frameworks.

11Monitoring, compliance and breaches
11.1Compliance with this policy is monitored by the Chief AI Officer and reported to the AI Governance Committee and Audit & Risk Committee.
11.2AI systems are subject to periodic review of performance, accuracy, bias and continued fitness for purpose.
11.3Suspected breaches, incidents or harms must be reported promptly through the agency's incident process.
11.4Breaches may result in withdrawal of access, disciplinary action, and mandatory reporting where required.
12Review

This policy is reviewed at least annually, and sooner where technology, legislation or whole-of-government direction changes materially. The Chief AI Officer is responsible for initiating each review.
