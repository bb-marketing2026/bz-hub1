Post 01 · The hook

When a pay decision is questioned — by an employee, a tribunal, an auditor or a remuneration committee — the question is almost never "is this number right?" It's "on what documented basis was it set?" Which framework, applied by whom, against what criteria, reviewed against what. Most HR leaders are confident their pay decisions are fair. Far fewer can produce the framework behind them on request. Free assessment on one REMassess domain — your choice, no pay data required.

Post 02 · Consistency

A test worth running on your own framework. If two managers set pay for comparable roles independently, would they apply the same framework and reach a similar outcome? If not, you don't have a fairness problem — you have a structure problem. And structure problems compound quietly, one appointment at a time, until someone puts two salaries side by side. Structured remuneration frameworks is one of five REMassess domains. Free assessment on that domain alone.

#Remuneration #HR #PeopleAndCulture #Governance

Post 03 · Evidencing equity

Here's a distinction that matters more than it sounds. Paying equitably and being able to demonstrate that you pay equitably are two different capabilities. Most organisations do the first. Far fewer can do the second on request — and it's the second that's tested when a figure becomes public, when an employee asks a direct question, or when a committee wants assurance rather than reassurance. Equity and benchmarking is one of five REMassess domains. Free assessment on just that one.

#PayEquity #HR #Remuneration #Governance

Post 04 · Performance link

Most organisations say reward is linked to performance. Fewer can show it from the record — that this year's outcomes actually followed measured performance rather than expectation, tenure, or the difficulty of the conversation that would have followed a lower rating. That gap isn't dishonesty. It's usually that the link exists in judgement rather than in documentation. Performance-linked compensation is one of five REMassess domains. Free assessment on one domain — comment "performance".

#PerformanceManagement #Remuneration #HR #Leadership

Post 05 · Not benchmarking

To be clear, because the assumption is fair: this isn't salary benchmarking. Market data tells you what other organisations pay. Useful, and you probably already buy it. What it doesn't tell you is whether your own framework is structured, compliant, equitable and applied consistently — which is the question you get asked internally, by your executive and by your people. Different question, different instrument. Free REMassess assessment on one domain of your choosing.

Post 06 · The restructure legacy

Every restructure leaves classification inconsistencies behind. Roles get merged, titles change, people are transitioned on existing terms, and two years later two people doing similar work sit on materially different pay — each entirely explicable at the time, and very difficult to explain now. Nobody did anything wrong. The framework just had no mechanism to reconcile it afterwards. Free REMassess assessment on one domain. No pay data required.

#HR #OrganisationalChange #Remuneration #Governance

Post 07 · The committee paper

If you present remuneration to a board or committee, you'll know the hardest question. Not "what are we paying?" — that's in the paper. It's "how do we know the framework is sound?" Market data doesn't answer it. A maturity position on your own remuneration governance does, and it changes the conversation from defending numbers to reporting on a managed system. Free assessment on one REMassess domain, confidential to you, ahead of your next committee paper.

Post 08 · Compliance drift

Remuneration compliance rarely fails suddenly. An award is varied, a superannuation rate changes, a classification structure is updated — and the framework catches most of it, because someone diligent reads the updates. Until that person changes roles. Tracked compliance turns individual diligence into a system. That distinction is invisible right up to the point where it isn't. Compliance management is one of five REMassess domains. Free assessment on one domain.

#Compliance #FairWork #Remuneration #HR

Post 09 · Capability vs evidence

A test that separates confident remuneration governance from defensible remuneration governance. Take any part of your pay framework you're comfortable about. Now ask: if an auditor or a committee asked us to prove this today, what would we hand over? A strong practice with weak evidence isn't good governance yet. It's good intentions waiting to be documented. REMassess scores each domain twice — how well it's embedded, and how readily you could prove it. Free assessment on one domain.

#Governance #Assurance #HR #Remuneration

Post 10 · Retention

When someone critical resigns and pay was part of the reason, the post-mortem usually focuses on the market rate. The more useful question is whether the framework would have surfaced the risk earlier. Was the drift visible in your analytics? Did anything flag that a key role had fallen behind, before the resignation rather than after it? Analytics and reporting is one of five REMassess domains, and the one most often missing.
