From register to roadmap

Turning maturity and diagnostic scores into a prioritised improvement plan a Board can actually track.

By Pat Scally · Chief Executive Officer, Blue Zoo

A maturity assessment or capability diagnostic produces a score. A score is a diagnosis, not a destination. The value comes from what happens next — converting a register of ratings into a short, sequenced, owned plan of improvement. Five steps make that translation reliable.

Plot every improvement the assessment surfaces on impact against effort. The grid does the arguing for you: quick wins build early momentum and credibility; major projects earn a place in the business plan; fill-ins wait for capacity; and low-value, high-effort items are named and parked rather than left to drift.

Prioritise ruthlessly. A roadmap of six well-chosen actions beats a list of thirty that no one owns.

Step 4 · the roadmap

Now, Next, Later — one page a Board can hold

Sequence the prioritised actions across three simple horizons. Each carries a named owner and a measure of done, so progress can be reported without a new system. The examples below are illustrative — replace them with your own.

Now · 0–3 months
Name owners for each significant governance domain
Close top repeat findings at root cause
Fix board-paper format to show options & evidence
Next · 3–9 months
Align assurance to the risk profile
Shift reporting from activity to outcomes
Build evidence where confidence lags capability
Later · 9–18 months
Embed improvement so it survives without a program
Benchmark maturity against peers
Re-measure and report the movement
Building the roadmap · checklist
✓Every action has one named owner
✓Every action has a measure of "done"
✓No more than six actions in the "Now" horizon
✓Each action traces back to an assessment gap
✓Quick wins are scheduled first for momentum
✓Evidence gaps are treated, not just capability gaps
✓A re-measure date is set before the roadmap starts
✓The whole plan fits on a single page for the Board

The test of a good roadmap: a director should be able to read it in two minutes, know who is accountable for each item, and see at the next review whether it moved. If it can't be tracked, it isn't a roadmap — it's a wish list.
