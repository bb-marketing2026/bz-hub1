Follow-up email — Chairs & Managing Directors

Sent 5–7 business days after the intro if there is no reply. Gives the six dimensions as standalone value, then lowers the ask.

Governance done differently
Subject: Re: The CEO appraisal — the six dimensions, for your own use
From: Pat Scally <pats@bluezoo.com.au>
To: [First name] [Last name], Chair, [Organisation]

Dear [First name],

Following up briefly on my note about assessing one dimension of chief-executive performance. Rather than ask again, here are the six dimensions themselves — useful as a structure for your own appraisal discussion, whether or not we ever speak:

  1. Corporate strategy. Vision, strategy and resource allocation. Does the CEO set a direction the board recognises, and fund it deliberately?
  2. Organisational alignment. Talent, culture and organisational design. Is the organisation shaped to deliver the strategy it has?
  3. Team and processes. Teamwork, decision‑making and management processes. How does the executive actually operate?
  4. Board engagement. Effectiveness, relationships and capability. Is what reaches the board sufficient to govern with?
  5. External stakeholders. Social purpose, interactions and moments of truth. How is the organisation experienced from outside?
  6. Personal work. Office environment, leadership model and personal perspective. The CEO's own practice and growth.

Two things tend to become apparent when a board rates these deliberately. The first is how much of the usual appraisal conversation concentrates on one or two dimensions and leaves the rest unexamined. The second is how differently directors rate the same chief executive — which is itself worth knowing before the conversation, not during it.

Two easy options, take either:

  • Send me the framework overview and I'll leave you to it — just reply “send it”.
  • Book the free assessment on a single dimension of your choosing, in confidence, with a 30‑minute debrief.

And if this isn't the right time, please just say so and I'll close the loop — no further follow‑ups.
